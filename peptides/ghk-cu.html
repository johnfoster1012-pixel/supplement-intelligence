GHK-Cu (copper peptide): what the human evidence shows

Evidence reviewed: November 25, 2026.

GHK-Cu has decades of lab and animal research but only a few small human trials, all of creams or serums. Injected GHK-Cu is not FDA-approved and has no published human trials, and the FDA flags safety concerns for compounded injections. This page is not medical advice. [FDA] [PMID 42797253]

No products are linked or recommended on this page.

TL;DR

  • GHK-Cu is a three-amino-acid peptide bound to copper. The body makes GHK, and skincare brands sell it as a copper peptide. [PMID 42787770]
  • Lab and animal studies consistently show effects on collagen, wound repair and inflammation. Human evidence is sparse: a 2026 review says it “does not meet contemporary active-entity quality standards.” [PMID 42797253]
  • For wrinkles, the human evidence is two small randomized trials, one of them negative on objective measures (grade B). [PMID 42619529] [PMID 16847171]
  • For hair loss and scars, there is no good trial of GHK-Cu on its own (grade C). [PMID 42976838] [PMID 42787770]
  • Injected GHK-Cu has no published human trials. The FDA says compounded injections may cause immune reactions and that human safety data are limited. [FDA]
  • About a third of the original research includes authors from a company that sells copper peptides. [PMID 42787770]

What is GHK-Cu?

GHK stands for glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine, a peptide made of three amino acids. It binds copper tightly, and the copper-bound form is called GHK-Cu. The body releases GHK when collagen and some other tissue proteins break down, and copper is needed by the enzymes that link new collagen together. [PMID 42787770]

It reaches people in two very different ways. Skincare brands sell creams and serums with copper peptides, sometimes listed as copper tripeptide-1. Separately, GHK-Cu is sold online as an injectable “research” peptide. The evidence and the rules are different for each. [PMID 39449909] [FDA]

What do lab and animal studies show?

A lot. A 2026 systematic review of 64 records describes more than four decades of lab and animal research on tissue repair, inflammation and antioxidant effects. A separate 2026 review says preclinical data “consistently support” effects on tissue remodeling, skin repair, inflammation and new blood vessel growth. Effects in cells and animals do not show what a cream or injection does in people. Grade C. [PMID 42787770] [PMID 42797253]

Who did the research matters too. The 2026 systematic review notes that about 35% of the original GHK-Cu studies in its window include Loren Pickart or Anna Margolina of Skin Biology Inc. as authors. Pickart first isolated the peptide and held commercial interests in the field for decades. [PMID 42787770]

Does GHK-Cu work for skin and wrinkles?

Possibly, but the trials are few and small. A 2026 systematic review in Aesthetic Surgery Journal found 20 studies of GHK-Cu on its own for aesthetic uses. Eighteen were lab or animal studies and two were randomized trials. [PMID 42619529]

A 2026 review of GHK-Cu chemistry adds that older cosmetic reports are “small or incompletely characterized,” meaning it is often unclear what form of the peptide a product contained. Its conclusion: GHK-Cu “should be regarded as a promising but unproven” treatment until controlled trials show it works. [PMID 42797253]

Does copper peptide help hair growth?

Not shown for GHK-Cu on its own. A 2026 systematic review of 14 human trials of peptides for hair loss gave its grade B to formulations containing GHK peptide, but those were combinations with other peptides, so the trials cannot show what GHK did. The 2026 chemistry review mentions one 18-person split-face eyebrow study with positive cosmetic results that did not define the form of GHK-Cu used. Grade C for hair loss claims about GHK-Cu alone. [PMID 42976838] [PMID 42797253]

Do copper peptides fade scars?

No one has shown that. A PubMed search on November 25, 2026 found no controlled trial of GHK-Cu alone for scars. A 2026 systematic review lists acne scarring among the uses where proper human trials are still needed. Grade C. [PMID 42787770]

What about injected GHK-Cu?

We found no published human trial of injected GHK-Cu. A 2026 review for orthopaedic and sports doctors says GHK-Cu “showed promise in wound healing and anti-inflammatory effects, but no clinical data support its use for musculoskeletal conditions,” and that dosing, frequency and length of treatment remain unknown. Grade C. [PMID 41476424]

The FDA. It lists “GHK-Cu (for injectable routes of administration)” in the “nominated but withdrawn” table of bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks in compounding (page current as of April 22, 2026). Its wording: “Compounded injectable drugs containing GHK-Cu may pose risk for immunogenicity due to the potential for aggregation and peptide-related impurities. There are limited data in humans to inform safety-related considerations.” Immunogenicity means the body may mount an immune reaction against the peptide. [FDA]

Trials under way. A phase 2 study of GHK-Cu for acute wounds (NCT07437586) was still recruiting with no results, according to a 2026 review. [PMID 42797253]

What are the side effects of GHK-Cu?

For creams and serums, the small trials above did not report safety problems in their abstracts, but they were too small to find uncommon ones. For injections, side effects in people are unknown because no trial has been published; the FDA flags possible immune reactions and impurities. Products sold online as “research use only” also raise questions about what is in the vial. See how research peptides differ from supplements and drugs. [FDA]

How should I read the evidence grades on this page?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, small, short, from a single trial, or that only measures a stand-in result (like a lab test) instead of the real outcome.
  • Grade C: animal, lab, uncontrolled or expert-opinion evidence, or no human evidence at all.

We grade each claim, not the compound. The skin claims here are grade B at best; hair, scar and injection claims are grade C.

Frequently asked questions

What is GHK-Cu?

GHK-Cu is a small peptide of three amino acids (glycine, histidine and lysine) bound to copper. The body makes GHK, and it is released when collagen breaks down. In skincare it is sold as a copper peptide, sometimes listed as copper tripeptide-1. It is also sold online as an injectable peptide. [PMID 42787770] [PMID 42797253]

Does GHK-Cu work for wrinkles and skin aging?

The human evidence is thin. A 2026 systematic review found only two randomized trials among 20 studies. In the one we could read, 13 people used skincare with or without GHK-Cu after laser resurfacing: blinded raters found no difference in redness, wrinkles or skin quality, though people using GHK-Cu were more satisfied. The other trial reported less wrinkle volume and depth than a control. That is grade B: small trials, not repeated. [PMID 42619529] [PMID 16847171]

Does copper peptide help hair growth?

Not shown for GHK-Cu on its own. A 2026 review of 14 hair trials rated some peptide formulations well, but the ones containing GHK were combinations with other peptides. A 2026 review of GHK-Cu itself mentions one 18-person eyebrow study with positive cosmetic results that did not define the product. Grade C for treating hair loss with GHK-Cu alone. [PMID 42976838] [PMID 42797253]

Do copper peptides fade scars?

No one has shown that. We found no controlled trial of GHK-Cu alone for scars in a PubMed search on November 25, 2026, and a 2026 systematic review lists acne scarring among the uses that still need proper trials (grade C). [PMID 42787770]

Is injectable GHK-Cu safe or FDA-approved?

It is not FDA-approved, and we found no published human trial of injected GHK-Cu. The FDA says compounded injectable GHK-Cu may cause an immune reaction because of clumping and peptide impurities, and that there are limited data in humans to judge safety. Grade C. [FDA] [PMID 41476424]

Do you need to cycle GHK-Cu or take breaks from it?

Nobody knows. A PubMed search on November 25, 2026 found no study comparing continuous use of GHK-Cu with cycles or breaks, so advice online about cycling is not based on trials. The few human trials tested creams and serums against a comparison product, not schedules. Grade C. [PMID 42619529]

Do GHK-Cu pills or capsules work?

That has not been tested. A PubMed search on November 25, 2026 found no human study of GHK-Cu taken by mouth. Research on delivering it has focused on the skin, and a 2025 review says even studies of carrying it into the skin in liposomes have received little attention. The only work on a swallowed form we found was a 2011 lab test of coated tablets designed to release GHK-Cu in the colon, with no people involved. Grade C. [PMID 39795193] [PMID 21457130]

Can GHK-Cu help eczema?

It has not been tested in people with eczema. The only study we found was a 2026 lab study in skin cells: a mix of GHK-Cu and a plant extract (Torilis japonica) lowered signals of the type of inflammation behind atopic eczema, and worked better than either alone. Results in cells do not show what a cream does on skin. Grade C. [PMID 42510549]
