TB-500: what the human evidence shows

Evidence reviewed: November 19, 2026.

TB-500 is not FDA-approved, has no published human trials, and is banned in sport. The FDA says it has not identified any human exposure data for it. This page is not medical advice. [FDA]

No products are linked or recommended on this page.

TL;DR

  • TB-500 is a lab-made, seven-amino-acid piece of a natural protein called thymosin beta-4. It is not the whole protein, and studies of the whole protein do not show what TB-500 does. [PMID 42937313]
  • No published human trial of TB-500 was found. A 2026 review of 80 studies found only one that tested TB-500 itself, and it was a lab study, not a healing study in people or animals. [Scoping review]
  • The main injury evidence is one study in 32 rats with repaired Achilles tendons. TB-500 rats had stronger tendons at four weeks. That is mostly animal studies (grade C). [PMID 42542926]
  • Side effects in people are unknown. The FDA flags possible immune reactions and impurities, and says it lacks information on whether TB-500 would cause harm in humans. [FDA]
  • Under an October 30, 2026 HHS policy, HHS says it will not act against licensed compounders who make TB-500 with a prescription under set conditions. The policy is interim and not FDA approval. [HHS]
  • TB-500 is on the World Anti-Doping Agency's list, banned in and out of competition. [WADA list, S2]

What is TB-500?

TB-500 is a short synthetic peptide: seven amino acids in the order LKKTETQ. That sequence is a small piece of thymosin beta-4, a protein the body makes on its own. The FDA's name for it is “Thymosin beta-4, fragment (LKKTETQ), also known as TB-500.” The HHS compounding policy describes the same seven-amino-acid sequence, with a small chemical cap on one end. [FDA] [HHS]

TB-500 is often sold or talked about as if it were thymosin beta-4. A 2026 review in the Journal of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons calls it “a distinct seven amino-acid fragment” and says no biological or clinical equivalence to the full protein has been shown. [PMID 42937313]

Has TB-500 been tested in people?

Not in any published trial we could find as of November 19, 2026. Three sources point the same way:

  • FDA: it “has not identified any human exposure data” for drug products containing the TB-500 fragment. [FDA]
  • A 2026 scoping review (a study that maps all the research on a topic) searched PubMed, Europe PMC and ClinicalTrials.gov through March 26, 2026 and included 80 studies. Seventy were on thymosin beta-4. One tested TB-500, and it looked at how TB-500 breaks down plus a wound test in lab cells. It found no human studies giving thymosin beta-4 or TB-500 for tendon, ligament, muscle, bone, cartilage or spine problems. [Scoping review]
  • The 2026 orthopaedic review found that none of these peptides has controlled human evidence for orthopaedic use and concluded that “routine clinical use cannot be currently recommended.” [PMID 42937313]

So any claim that TB-500 heals injuries, cuts recovery time or reduces inflammation in people has no human evidence behind it. Grade C.

What do animal studies show?

The clearest test is a 2026 study in 32 rats whose Achilles tendons were cut and repaired. Eight rats each got nothing, BPC-157, TB-500, or both, for four weeks. [PMID 42542926] [Journal abstract]

  • TB-500: the repaired tendons took more force to break than in untreated rats, and tissue scores were better.
  • BPC-157: tendons were a little stronger, but the difference could have been chance.
  • Both together: no extra benefit over either one alone.

The authors call it an exploratory rat model study. Rats got the peptide by injection into the belly, and the study ended at four weeks. A rat tendon isn't your tendon. Grade C.

What about thymosin beta-4 studies in people?

The full protein has been tested in a few small human trials, mostly for eye and skin problems. These trials did not use TB-500, and a 2026 review says results from them “cannot establish musculoskeletal efficacy or safety.” [PMID 42937313] [Scoping review]

What are the side effects of TB-500?

No one has measured them, because TB-500 has not been studied in people. Here is what official sources and reviews say about the risks.

The FDA. It lists TB-500 in the “nominated but withdrawn” table of bulk drug substances that may present significant safety risks in compounding (page current as of April 22, 2026). It says compounded TB-500 may cause an immune reaction (immunogenicity) for some routes because the peptide can clump together, and may contain peptide impurities. It also says it “lacks important information regarding any safety issues raised by this drug, including whether it would cause harm if administered to humans.” [FDA]

HHS. The October 2026 compounding policy takes a softer view. It says the safety concerns FDA's drug center raised “are inferential rather than observed,” meaning predicted rather than seen in people, and points to the parent protein's human safety record. That record is for the full protein, not for TB-500. [HHS]

What is in the vial. The orthopaedic review warns that products sold as TB-500 raise concerns about identity, purity, strength, sterility and manufacturing consistency. Grade C (expert review). [PMID 42937313]

Cancer questions. Lab and mouse research has linked the body's own thymosin beta-4 to cancer spread, and in one analysis high levels were tied to worse outcomes in several human cancers. That research is about the natural protein, not a test of TB-500. No study shows TB-500 causes or speeds up cancer in people, and none shows it doesn't. Grade C. [PMID 29330296]

Is TB-500 FDA-approved or legal?

TB-500 is not FDA-approved for any use. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved either: the FDA does not check their safety, effectiveness or quality before they are sold. [FDA] [HHS]

On October 30, 2026, HHS published an enforcement-discretion policy for six peptides: BPC-157, Epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax and TB-500. It says HHS does not intend to act against licensed compounders who make them under set conditions while formal rules are written. [HHS] [Federal Register] For TB-500 those conditions include:

  • a prescription for a specific patient,
  • injection under the skin or into muscle only,
  • raw peptide from an FDA-registered manufacturer with a certificate of analysis,
  • reporting serious side effects and quality problems to the FDA.

The Federal Register notice calls the policy a draft and an interim policy, and says it “does not establish any rights for any person and is not binding on HHS or the public.” HHS can revisit it if a safety or quality problem appears. It is not FDA approval. It covers licensed pharmacies and doctors only, not peptides sold online as “research use” products. [Federal Register] [HHS]

Is TB-500 banned in sports?

Yes. On the World Anti-Doping Agency's 2026 Prohibited List, class S2 names “Thymosin-ß4 and its derivatives, e.g., TB-500.” S2 substances are prohibited at all times, in and out of competition. [WADA list, S2] USADA's guide to the 2027 list, which takes effect January 1, 2027, again lists TB-500 as prohibited under S2. [USADA] The orthopaedic review tells doctors to raise anti-doping rules with athletes who ask about these peptides. [PMID 42937313]

TB-500 vs BPC-157: how do they compare?

They are different peptides that are often sold together. Neither is FDA-approved, and neither has controlled human trials for injuries. Both are on the HHS compounding list. BPC-157 has three tiny uncontrolled human reports; TB-500 has none. The only head-to-head test is the 32-rat tendon study above. Details are on our BPC-157 side effects page. [PMIDs 42937313, 42542926] [HHS]

How should I read the evidence grades on this page?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, small, short or from a single trial.
  • Grade C: animal, lab, uncontrolled or expert-opinion evidence, or no human evidence at all.

We grade each claim, not the compound. Every claim about TB-500 itself on this page is grade C. The two grade B rows are about the full thymosin beta-4 protein, not TB-500.

Frequently asked questions

What is TB-500?

TB-500 is a lab-made, seven-amino-acid piece of a natural protein called thymosin beta-4 (sequence LKKTETQ). It is often sold or described as if it were the whole protein, but no study has shown the two act the same way in people. [PMID 42937313] [FDA]

Does TB-500 work in people?

Nobody knows. No published human trial of TB-500 was found, and the FDA says it has not identified any human exposure data for it. The injury evidence is animal research, led by one 32-rat tendon study (grade C). [PMID 42542926] [FDA]

What are the side effects of TB-500?

They are unknown because TB-500 has not been studied in people. The FDA flags a possible immune reaction to the peptide and problems with impurities. Products sold online also raise concerns about what is really in the vial and whether it is sterile. [FDA] [PMID 42937313]

Is TB-500 FDA-approved or legal?

TB-500 is not FDA-approved for any use. An October 30, 2026 HHS policy lists TB-500 among six peptides that HHS says it will not act against licensed compounders for making, with a prescription and under set conditions, while rules are written. That policy is interim, not binding, and not FDA approval. [HHS] [Federal Register]

Is TB-500 banned in sports?

Yes. The World Anti-Doping Agency lists thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives, such as TB-500, under class S2, which is banned at all times, in and out of competition. USADA lists TB-500 as prohibited on the 2027 list too. [WADA list, S2] [USADA]

Is TB-500 the same as thymosin beta-4?

No. Thymosin beta-4 is a full natural protein that has been tested in small human trials for eye and skin problems. TB-500 is a short fragment of it. A 2026 review says there is no shown biological or clinical equivalence between them, so results for the full protein do not carry over to TB-500. [PMID 42937313]
