BPC-157: what the human evidence actually shows

BPC-157 is not FDA-approved, and human evidence is very thin. Most evidence is animal or laboratory research. Small uncontrolled reports do not establish that it works or is safe in people. This page is not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. BPC-157 is not an FDA-approved drug. A draft enforcement-discretion policy is not FDA approval and is not the same as adding a substance to the 503A bulks list. [FDA; HHS Federal Register notice]

What human evidence exists for BPC-157?

Human evidence is very thin. Published reports reviewed here include an uncontrolled knee-pain chart review, an uncontrolled interstitial-cystitis pilot, and an intravenous safety pilot in 2 previously exposed adults. These are Grade C evidence, not adequate proof of benefit or safety. [PMIDs 34324435, 39325560, 40131143]

Does BPC-157 help injuries or inflammation in people?

That is not established by rigorous human trials in the evidence reviewed. Animal and laboratory findings cannot prove injury healing or anti-inflammatory benefit in people. The human reports are small and uncontrolled. [PMIDs 40756949, 40789979]

What has FDA said about compounding BPC-157?

FDA states: “Compounded drugs containing BPC-157 may pose risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration and may have complexities with regard to peptide-related impurities and active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) characterization. FDA has identified no, or only limited, safety-related information for the proposed routes of administration. Therefore, the agency lacks sufficient information to know whether the drug would cause harm when administered to humans.” [FDA]

How does BPC-157 compare with TB-500?

A rigorous human comparison is not established in the evidence reviewed. There is no adequate published human head-to-head trial here. TB-500 is often used as a name for a thymosin beta-4 fragment and is also not FDA-approved based on these sources. [FDA; evidence reviewed on this page]

What is known about BPC-157 side effects?

Too little is known to define a human safety profile. Small reports describing few or no adverse events cannot establish safety. FDA cites limited safety information, immunogenicity risk for certain routes, and complexities involving peptide-related impurities and API characterization. [FDA; PMIDs 40756949, 40131143]

How to read the evidence

  • Grade A: a published Phase 3 randomized trial, or a nutrient systematic review of randomized trials.
  • Grade B: Phase 2 trials, exploratory analyses, small human pharmacokinetic studies, or cohorts.
  • Grade C: uncontrolled or retrospective reports, animal or laboratory inference, and unmatched cross-trial comparisons.

The human BPC-157 benefit and safety reports reviewed here are Grade C. A systematic review dominated by animal work does not earn Grade A for human claims.

What BPC-157 is

BPC-157, or Body Protection Compound-157, is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide related to a sequence described from gastric juice. Reviews describe it as investigational; proposed mechanisms do not establish clinical benefit. [PMIDs 40756949, 40789979, 40005999]

What the human record actually contains

A review dominated by preclinical studies

Vasireddi searched through June 3, 2024 and included 36 studies: 35 preclinical and one clinical. It found no clinical safety data. Animal injury models and proposed angiogenesis, growth-hormone receptor, and cytokine mechanisms cannot establish human injury treatment. Grade C for human-benefit claims. [PMID 40756949]

Knee pain: a retrospective chart review

A single Orlando clinic reached 16 of 17 patients by phone; 12 received intra-articular BPC-157 alone. There was no control, standardized functional or quality-of-life assessment, or fixed follow-up. Seven of 12 later reported relief beyond six months, but telephone reports cannot establish causation. Grade C. [PMIDs 34324435, 40756949]

Interstitial cystitis: an uncontrolled pilot

Twelve women unresponsive to pentosan polysulfate received BPC-157 around bladder inflammation during cystoscopy, using product from a 503A pharmacy. Ten reported complete resolution and 100% success; two rated success 80%, with no adverse events reported. The study was small, unblinded, uncontrolled, and from the same clinic group. Grade C. [PMID 39325560]

Intravenous safety: too small to define risk

The pilot involved two previously exposed adults over two infusion days. Tested cardiac, liver, kidney, thyroid, and glucose biomarkers did not measurably change and no side effects were reported. This design cannot establish safety. Grade C. [PMID 40131143]

Later reviews call human data extremely limited and large rigorous trials lacking; reviews of weak reports do not fill the controlled-trial gap. [PMIDs 40789979, 40005999]

FDA status and exact safety language

The FDA page, current April 22, 2026 and retrieved November 11, 2026, places BPC-157 in “Bulk drug substances nominated but withdrawn,” not its current category 2 table.

Compounded drugs containing BPC-157 may pose risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration and may have complexities with regard to peptide-related impurities and active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) characterization. FDA has identified no, or only limited, safety-related information for the proposed routes of administration. Therefore, the agency lacks sufficient information to know whether the drug would cause harm when administered to humans.

Source: FDA: Certain Bulk Drug Substances for Use in Compounding that May Present Significant Safety Risks. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved, and FDA does not review their safety, effectiveness, or quality before marketing.

What the HHS draft notice does—and does not establish

The October 30, 2026 Federal Register notice announced a draft enforcement-discretion policy for specified 503A compounders using peptide substances in Appendix A, possible future rulemaking, and possible reconsideration if risks arise. It “does not establish any rights for any person and is not binding on HHS or the public.” It is not approval or addition to the 503A bulks list.

This review did not verify Appendix A because the official policy PDF could not be retrieved, and therefore makes no claim that it lists BPC-157. The notice supplies no efficacy or safety evidence.

BPC-157 versus TB-500

No adequate published human head-to-head trial is present. TB-500 commonly names a thymosin beta-4 fragment and is also not FDA-approved based on these sources.

What remains unknown

Tiny reports cannot define common side effects, rare harms, long-term outcomes, or dependable benefits. Absence of reported adverse events is not a safety profile. FDA explicitly says it lacks enough information to know whether compounded BPC-157 would cause harm.
