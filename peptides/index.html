Peptides for weight loss, healing and skin: what the evidence shows

Short answer. “Peptide” covers very different things. Some are drugs tested in Phase 3 trials. Others are sold with almost no human evidence at all.

  • BPC-157 is not FDA-approved. Most of its research is in animals, and the few human reports are tiny and uncontrolled (grade C).
  • TB-500 is not FDA-approved and has no published human trials. The injury evidence is animal research (grade C), and it is banned in sport.
  • Retatrutide is an unapproved drug that has been tested in Phase 3 randomized trials. Those trials found large average weight loss (grade A, because more than one randomized trial agrees). It cannot be compounded under federal law.
  • Peptide supplements, like collagen, are not approved by the FDA for safety or effectiveness before sale. How supplements, drugs and “research” peptides differ.
  • Nothing on this page is advice to use, buy or dose any peptide. Talk to a clinician first.

Do peptides work for weight loss?

It depends on which peptide. The strong weight-loss evidence is for peptide drugs tested in randomized trials, not for peptides sold as supplements or “research” products.

  • Retatrutide, an investigational peptide given as a weekly injection, produced large average weight loss in Phase 3 trials (grade A). It is not FDA-approved. What the trials found.
  • Semaglutide is a peptide drug that the FDA tested and approved before it was sold. How peptide drugs differ from peptide supplements, and how semaglutide and tirzepatide trials compare.
  • BPC-157, TB-500 and GHK-Cu: none of the human studies we found for them tested weight loss. Their evidence is below.
  • Salmon peptides sold as a natural GLP-1 activator: the claim that they work like a GLP-1 drug is not established. Details.

What does the evidence show for BPC-157?

Very little in people. A 2025 review of BPC-157 for sports and bone, joint and muscle injuries looked at 36 studies. Thirty-five were animal or lab studies and one was in people. It found no clinical safety data.

The human reports are small and had no comparison group:

  • Knee pain. One clinic looked back at past patients and asked them by phone how their knees felt after the injections. Twelve had received BPC-157 alone. Phone calls like these cannot show what the shots did.
  • Bladder pain (interstitial cystitis). Twelve women were treated in a pilot with no comparison group and no blinding.
  • Safety. An intravenous safety test involved just 2 adults over two days. That is far too few to judge risk.

All of this is grade C: mostly animal studies, plus uncontrolled reports. FDA lists BPC-157 in its “nominated but withdrawn” table of bulk drug substances for compounding, and says it “lacks sufficient information to know whether the drug would cause harm when administered to humans.” What is and isn’t known about its side effects, with FDA’s exact wording, is on our BPC-157 page.

What does the evidence show for TB-500?

Even less than BPC-157 in people. TB-500 is a short lab-made piece of thymosin beta-4, a protein the body makes. The FDA says it has not identified any human exposure data for TB-500, and a 2026 review of 80 studies found only one that tested TB-500 itself. That study tracked how TB-500 breaks down in lab tests and in rats, and tested it on cells in a dish. It did not test healing in people.

The main injury study used 32 rats with repaired Achilles tendons. Rats given TB-500 had stronger tendons at four weeks. That is grade C. Small human studies of the full thymosin beta-4 protein, mostly for eye and skin problems, do not show what TB-500 does.

Under an October 30, 2026 HHS policy, HHS says it will not act against licensed compounders who make TB-500 with a prescription under set conditions; the policy is interim and not FDA approval. TB-500 is on the World Anti-Doping Agency’s list, banned in and out of competition. Sources and FDA’s wording are on our TB-500 page.

What does the evidence show for retatrutide?

Much more. Retatrutide has published Phase 3 randomized trials. In TRIUMPH-1, adults without diabetes lost 17.6% to 25.0% of their body weight on average over 80 weeks, depending on dose. People given placebo lost 3.9%. That is grade A for weight loss, because other randomized trials agree: TRIUMPH-2, in adults with type 2 diabetes, and an earlier Phase 2 trial also found much more weight loss than placebo. It is not a prediction for any one person.

Stomach and bowel problems were the most common side effects. TRIUMPH-2 also reported low blood pressure and altered skin sensation, and Phase 2 reported faster heart rates.

Two limits matter. No head-to-head trial against tirzepatide had been published as of November 15, 2026, so numbers from separate trials cannot show which drug works better. And retatrutide is still not approved: FDA says it has not been found safe and effective for any condition. Side effects and the full trial list are on our retatrutide page. The doses each trial used are on retatrutide dosing schedule, and the separate trials are set side by side in tirzepatide vs retatrutide.

Are “research use only” peptides the same as the ones in trials?

No one can tell from the label. FDA has warned companies that illegally sold unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled as being for research purposes, and says these products are of unknown quality. For BPC-157, FDA points to possible immune reactions, impurities and limited safety information. Trial results do not make products sold online reliable.

What about peptides sold as supplements?

  • Salmon peptides (salmon protein hydrolysate) are marketed in some products as a natural GLP-1 activator. The only published human trial of it that we found is a small pilot in 14 adults. A separate trial of a different salmon protein in 93 adults found it did not change blood sugar or insulin levels. The claim that it works like a GLP-1 drug is not established. See Do GLP-1 supplements work?
  • Collagen peptides have mixed results that depend on the outcome. A review of 23 skin trials found the benefit disappeared when only higher-quality or non-industry-funded trials were counted. See our collagen evidence page.

How do we grade the evidence?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome (more than one randomized trial agreeing, or a pooled analysis of consistent trials).
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or that only measures a stand-in result (like a lab or imaging test) instead of how people feel or function.
  • Grade C: animal, lab, cell, mechanistic, uncontrolled, anecdotal or expert-opinion evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence at all.

We grade each claim, not the compound. Retatrutide’s weight-loss result is grade A, but a claim that it beats tirzepatide is grade C because no trial has compared them directly.

Common questions

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. BPC-157 is not an FDA-approved drug. An October 30, 2026 draft HHS policy on compounding certain peptides is not FDA approval.

Does BPC-157 work in people?

That has not been shown. The human reports are tiny and had no comparison group: 12 knee-pain patients reached by phone, 12 women with a bladder condition, and 2 adults in a safety test. Most of the research is in animals (grade C).

Does TB-500 work in people?

Nobody knows. No published human trial of TB-500 was found, and the FDA says it has not identified any human exposure data for it. The injury evidence comes from animal research, led by one 32-rat tendon study (grade C).

Is retatrutide approved?

No. Retatrutide is investigational. FDA says it has not been found safe and effective for any condition and cannot be used in compounding under federal law.

How much weight did people lose on retatrutide in trials?

In the TRIUMPH-1 trial, adults without diabetes lost 17.6% to 25.0% of their body weight on average over 80 weeks, depending on dose, against 3.9% with placebo (grade A, because other randomized trials of retatrutide agree). An average is not a prediction for one person.

Are “research peptides” sold online safe?

Nobody can say. FDA has warned companies that illegally sold unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled as being for research purposes, and says these products are of unknown quality. For compounded BPC-157, FDA says it lacks enough information to know whether it would cause harm in people.

Is it legal to buy peptides like BPC-157 in the US?

It depends on how it is sold, and the federal rules shifted in 2026. BPC-157 is not an FDA-approved drug, and it is not on the DEA's list of controlled substances (November 2026 edition). FDA lists it among compounding substances whose nomination was withdrawn, and says it lacks enough information to know whether it would cause harm in people. A draft HHS policy published October 30, 2026, which HHS says is not binding, says HHS does not intend to take enforcement action against state-licensed pharmacies, federal facilities or licensed doctors that compound with the six peptides listed in its appendix: BPC-157, Epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax and TB-500. For GLP-1 peptides such as retatrutide, FDA says companies selling them labeled “for research purposes” or “not for human consumption” did so illegally. This covers the US federal position only, not state law, and is not legal advice.

What about AOD-9604, CJC-1295 and MOTS-c?

None is FDA-approved, and FDA lists all three among compounding substances whose nomination was withdrawn. For each, FDA says compounded versions may provoke an immune reaction and raise concerns about impurities. It says it has identified serious adverse events that may be linked to AOD-9604, though cause is not clear; serious adverse events with CJC-1295, including a raised heart rate and a whole-body reaction in which blood vessels widen, with limited clinical data; and no human exposure data at all for MOTS-c. MOTS-c is one of the six peptides in the October 30, 2026 draft HHS compounding policy, which covers it only as an injection under the skin or into muscle; AOD-9604 and CJC-1295 are not on that list. A 2026 sports medicine review says rigorous human safety data for unapproved peptides like these are scarce (grade C).

Do salmon peptide or collagen peptide supplements work?

It depends on the claim. The claim that salmon peptides work like a GLP-1 drug is not established. Collagen peptide results are mixed and depend on the outcome measured.
