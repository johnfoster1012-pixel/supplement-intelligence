Peptides: which ones have human evidence

Short answer. "Peptide" covers three very different things: approved peptide drugs, unapproved research peptides, and supplements with "peptide" on the label. The evidence behind each is not the same, so each one is reviewed on its own page.

This page is a guide to those reviews. It is not medical advice, and it does not cover dosing or where to buy anything.

What is a peptide?

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids, the same building blocks that make up proteins. That is a description of a molecule's shape, not of what it does. Some peptides are prescription drugs with large trials behind them. Others have only been studied in animals or cells.

What are the three kinds?

Approved peptide drugs

Semaglutide and tirzepatide are peptide drugs that the FDA has approved for specific uses. They have large randomized trials in people. We cover what those trials measured in the GLP-1 guides.

How do we grade peptide claims?

We grade the specific claim, not the compound.

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, or from a single trial.
  • Grade C: animal, cell, or anecdotal evidence, or no human evidence.

One peptide can earn different grades for different claims. How evidence is reviewed.

What should you check before believing a peptide claim?

  • Was it tested in people, or only in animals or cells?
  • How many people, for how long, and against a placebo?
  • Is it an approved drug, or sold as "for research use"?
  • Does the claim match what the study measured?
