Peptides: what the evidence shows

Short answer. “Peptide” covers very different things. Some are drugs tested in Phase 3 trials. Others are sold with almost no human evidence at all.

  • BPC-157 is not FDA-approved. Most of its research is in animals, and the few human reports are tiny and uncontrolled (grade C).
  • Retatrutide is an unapproved drug that has been tested in Phase 3 randomized trials. Those trials found large average weight loss (grade A within those trials). It cannot be compounded under federal law.
  • Nothing on this page is advice to use, buy or dose any peptide. Talk to a clinician first.

What does the evidence show for BPC-157?

Very little in people. One review searched for studies through June 2024 and found 36. Thirty-five were animal or lab studies and one was in people. It found no clinical safety data.

The human reports are small and had no comparison group:

  • Knee pain. One clinic phoned past patients. Twelve had received BPC-157 injections alone, and 7 of them later said their pain eased for more than six months. Phone reports cannot show the shot caused it.
  • Bladder pain (interstitial cystitis). Twelve women were treated in a pilot with no comparison group and no blinding.
  • Safety. An intravenous safety test involved just 2 adults over two days. That is far too few to judge risk.

All of this is grade C: mostly animal studies, plus uncontrolled reports. FDA lists BPC-157 in its “nominated but withdrawn” table of bulk drug substances for compounding, and says it “lacks sufficient information to know whether the drug would cause harm when administered to humans.” The full review, with FDA’s exact wording and the 2026 HHS draft policy, is on our BPC-157 page.

What does the evidence show for retatrutide?

Much more. Retatrutide has published Phase 3 randomized trials. In TRIUMPH-1, adults without diabetes lost 17.6% to 25.0% of their body weight on average over 80 weeks, depending on dose. People given placebo lost 3.9%. That is grade A for weight loss within that trial. It is not a prediction for any one person.

Stomach and bowel problems were the most common side effects. TRIUMPH-2 also reported low blood pressure and altered skin sensation, and Phase 2 reported faster heart rates.

Two limits matter. No head-to-head trial against tirzepatide had been published as of November 10, 2026, so numbers from separate trials cannot show which drug works better. And retatrutide is still not approved: FDA says it has not been found safe and effective for any condition. Doses, side effects and the full trial list are on our retatrutide page.

Are “research use only” peptides the same as the ones in trials?

No one can tell from the label. FDA has warned about illegal online sales of unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research use, and says their quality is unknown. For BPC-157, FDA points to possible immune reactions, impurities and limited safety information. Trial results do not make products sold online reliable.

What about peptides sold as supplements?

  • Salmon peptides (salmon protein hydrolysate) are marketed in some products as a natural GLP-1 activator. The only published human trial of it is a small pilot in 14 adults. An independent trial of a different salmon protein in 93 adults found it did not change blood sugar or insulin levels. The claim that it works like a GLP-1 drug is not established. See Do GLP-1 supplements work?
  • Collagen peptides have mixed results that depend on the outcome. A review of 23 skin trials found the benefit disappeared when only higher-quality or non-industry-funded trials were counted. See our collagen evidence page.

How do we grade the evidence?

  • Grade A: a published Phase 3 randomized trial, for the outcomes reported in that trial.
  • Grade B: smaller or earlier human evidence, such as Phase 2 trials, exploratory analyses or small human studies.
  • Grade C: uncontrolled or retrospective reports, animal or lab findings, and comparisons between separate trials.

We grade each claim, not the compound. Retatrutide’s weight-loss result is grade A, but a claim that it beats tirzepatide is grade C because no trial has compared them directly.

Common questions

Is BPC-157 FDA-approved?

No. BPC-157 is not an FDA-approved drug. An October 30, 2026 draft HHS policy on compounding certain peptides is not FDA approval.

Does BPC-157 work in people?

That has not been shown. The human reports are tiny and had no comparison group: 12 knee-pain patients reached by phone, 12 women with a bladder condition, and 2 adults in a safety test. Most of the research is in animals (grade C).

Is retatrutide approved?

No. Retatrutide is investigational. FDA says it has not been found safe and effective for any condition and cannot be used in compounding under federal law.

How much weight did people lose on retatrutide in trials?

In the TRIUMPH-1 trial, adults without diabetes lost 17.6% to 25.0% of their body weight on average over 80 weeks, depending on dose, against 3.9% with placebo (grade A within that trial). An average is not a prediction for one person.

Are “research peptides” sold online safe?

Nobody can say. FDA has warned about illegal online sales of unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research use, and says their quality is unknown. For compounded BPC-157, FDA says it lacks enough information to know whether it would cause harm in people.

Do salmon peptide or collagen peptide supplements work?

It depends on the claim. The claim that salmon peptides work like a GLP-1 drug is not established. Collagen peptide results are mixed and depend on the outcome measured.
