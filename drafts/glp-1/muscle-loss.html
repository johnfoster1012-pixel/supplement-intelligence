Does GLP-1 use cause muscle loss? What the trials actually show

Evidence reviewed: November 11, 2026.

Fat mass and lean or muscle-related mass can both fall during substantial weight loss on incretin medicines. In some analyses, the lean share is similar to diet, lifestyle, or placebo groups, so it is not automatically a unique drug effect. Objective strength and physical-function outcomes were often not measured: a lower scan-based lean-mass number is not a proven loss of strength or daily function. DXA, BIA, CT, MRI, and definitions of lean, fat-free, or muscle mass differ; these reviews cannot be averaged as one trial.

Frequently asked questions

Does GLP-1 use cause muscle loss?
Lean or muscle-related tissue can fall along with fat during GLP-1-related weight loss. In a 35-trial review, the median share of weight loss from muscle-related measures was 28.3%, with a wide range. [PMID 41996180]

Is that lean-mass change unique to these drugs?
Not clearly. In a SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy, about 75% of the weight lost was fat and 25% was lean mass in both the tirzepatide and placebo groups. A later review found a similar lean-mass share of weight loss with incretin medicines and lifestyle programs. [PMIDs 39996356, 41877354]

How much lean mass is typically lost?
Estimates vary by method and trial. One review of 22 trials found an average lean-mass change of −0.86 kg, about 25% of total weight loss, while the relative lean-mass percentage was unchanged. Another analysis estimated that about 30% of the weight lost was lean mass (−1.9 kg versus placebo). [PMIDs 39719170, 39710882]

Does muscle function get worse?
That is not established here. The 35-trial review found no study that reported objective physical-function outcomes. A drop in measured lean mass is not the same as a measured loss of strength or daily function. [PMID 41996180]

Is tirzepatide different from semaglutide for muscle?
A clear ranking is not established. One review estimated lean mass as 35.2% of the weight lost with semaglutide and 25.4% with tirzepatide, but the trials were not a single head-to-head comparison. [PMID 41877354]

Can exercise change the lean-mass share?
This page does not give a training plan. In one pooled analysis, lifestyle programs plus resistance training had a lower share of weight lost as lean mass (17.5%) than incretin medicines or lifestyle programs alone. That is a study finding, not personal advice. [PMID 41877354]

How to read the evidence

  • Grade A: published Phase 3 randomized trials and systematic reviews, for reported outcomes.
  • Grade B: exploratory, nested, post hoc, subgroup, observational, or Phase 2 evidence.
  • Grade C: uncontrolled reports, mechanistic predictions, and comparisons between unmatched trials.

The systematic reviews are Grade A only for their reported body-composition outcomes; nested and post hoc analyses are Grade B. Function and indirect drug rankings require separate evidence.

What the broad Annals review measured

Batsis and colleagues reviewed 35 randomized studies of incretin medicines. Median duration was 26 weeks and median enrollment was 78 participants. Only 42.9% were at low risk of bias, and 10 studies prespecified body composition as a primary outcome. Weight loss was consistently greater with incretin medicines and accompanied by lower total fat mass and visceral adiposity. Grade A for body-composition proportions, not function.

The median share of total weight loss attributed to muscle-related indices was 28.3% (IQR 15.9% to 39.9%). For BIA or DXA studies it was about 29%; for CT or MRI about 25.3%, using a different benchmark. These measures are not interchangeable. No included study reported objective physical-function outcomes. [PMID 41996180]

A correction was published; this page does not infer what changed. [PMID 42258831]

What the other pooled analyses found

A 22-trial analysis of 2,258 participants found pooled changes of −3.55 kg total weight, −2.95 kg fat mass, and −0.86 kg lean mass. Lean mass accounted for approximately 25% of weight loss while relative lean mass was unchanged. [PMID 39719170]

A 20-trial analysis of 15,782 participants estimated lean mass as 35.2% of weight lost with semaglutide, 25.4% with tirzepatide, and 26.8% with liraglutide. Lifestyle programs averaged 26.2%; the drug figures are not a head-to-head ranking. Programs plus resistance training averaged 17.5%, a study result rather than a prescription. [PMID 41877354]

Nine placebo-controlled trials estimated weight change of −6.9 kg and lean-mass change of −1.9 kg versus placebo, approximately 30% of weight lost. Bone-density findings need more data. [PMID 39710882]

Tirzepatide: a DXA substudy and an MRI analysis

The SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy included 160 participants. At week 72, weight, fat mass, and lean mass changed by −21.3%, −33.9%, and −10.9% with pooled tirzepatide versus −5.3%, −8.2%, and −2.6% with placebo. About 75% of weight lost was fat and 25% lean in both groups. Grade B: nested substudy. [PMID 39996356]

SURPASS-3 MRI was a post hoc analysis in 246 people with type 2 diabetes. Pooled tirzepatide was associated with lower muscle fat infiltration (−0.36 percentage points) and muscle volume (−0.64 L) versus an insulin comparator. This does not establish protected strength or improved function. [PMID 40318682]

Bottom line

Measured lean or muscle-related tissue can decline alongside fat. The size and meaning depend on the method and comparator. The evidence does not establish a unique drug effect, dependable drug ranking, or measured decline in strength. It does not provide an exercise or treatment plan.
