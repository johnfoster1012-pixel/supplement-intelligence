Magnesium L-threonate vs glycinate: what studies actually compare

A large direct magnesium L-threonate-versus-glycinate outcomes trial is missing, so a better form is not established. Human L-threonate trials tested a trademarked product against placebo and produced mixed sleep findings. The widely cited brain-magnesium study was in rats; it cannot establish human benefit or superiority over glycinate.

Frequently asked questions

Is magnesium L-threonate better than glycinate?

That is not established. A large direct L-threonate-versus-glycinate outcomes trial is missing from the evidence reviewed.

What has L-threonate been studied for?

Human trials have tested a trademarked L-threonate product for sleep and cognition versus placebo, not versus glycinate. A widely cited 2010 paper was in rats, which does not establish a human benefit. [PMIDs 39252819, 41601871, 20152124]

Did L-threonate improve sleep?

Results are mixed. A 21-day trial reported better objective sleep-stage and readiness scores versus placebo on a wearable device. A 6-week trial found a better score for self-reported sleep-related impairment, but no group differences on several other sleep questionnaires or on wearable sleep outcomes. [PMIDs 39252819, 41601871]

Did it improve cognition?

A 6-week trial reported a better NIH Total Cognition Composite (p=0.043) and reaction time (p=0.031) versus placebo, and a 7.5-year reduction in the authors’ estimated brain cognitive age. There was no difference on Raven’s Progressive Matrices. [PMID 41601871]

What did glycinate show for sleep?

A bisglycinate trial found a small sleep-symptom improvement versus placebo. That trial did not include L-threonate, so it is not a form comparison. [PMID 40918053]

What amounts were studied?

One trial used 1 g/day of magnesium L-threonate for 21 days; another used 2 g daily of a Magtein product for 6 weeks; a bisglycinate trial used 250 mg elemental magnesium daily. These are research protocols, not personal instructions. Product grams are not the same as elemental magnesium. [PMIDs 39252819, 41601871, 40918053]

How to read the evidence

  • Grade A: Phase 3 trials or nutrient systematic reviews of randomized trials.
  • Grade B: Phase 2 trials, exploratory analyses, small human studies, and cohorts.
  • Grade C: uncontrolled reports, animal or laboratory inference, and unmatched cross-trial comparisons.

The placebo-controlled product trials are Grade B; the rat study and indirect form-superiority claims are Grade C.

The brain-magnesium claim began with animal work

Slutsky and colleagues reported raised brain magnesium and better learning and memory measures in rats. It was not a human trial and did not compare glycinate. Grade C for inferred human benefit. [PMID 20152124]

A short L-threonate sleep trial

Hausenblas randomized 80 adults aged 35–55 with self-assessed sleep problems to 1 g/day of trademarked L-threonate or placebo for 21 days. Differences included Oura-ring deep sleep, REM, light-sleep, activity and readiness measures, plus several subjective daytime findings. This short product-specific study does not show superiority over glycinate. Grade B. [PMID 39252819]

A six-week trial: cognition improved on some measures

Lopresti and Smith randomized 100 adults aged 18–45 with dissatisfied sleep to 2 g daily Magtein or placebo. NIH Total Cognition Composite improved more (p=0.043), as did reaction time (p=0.031); Raven's Progressive Matrices did not (p=0.953). The authors' 7.5-year estimated “brain cognitive age” reduction does not prove aging reversal. Grade B. [PMID 41601871]

Sleep results were mixed

Sleep-related impairment improved (p=0.043), but sleep disturbance (p=0.316), restorative sleep (p=0.439), wellbeing (p=0.436), and wearable sleep outcomes did not differ. Resting heart rate fell more (p=0.030) and sleep HRV rose more (p=0.036). [PMID 41601871]

What the bisglycinate trial adds

Schuster tested 250 mg elemental bisglycinate daily versus placebo. Insomnia Severity Index changes were −3.9 versus −2.3, a small effect (d=0.2). It did not include L-threonate, so cross-trial comparison is Grade C. [PMID 40918053]

Product weight is not elemental magnesium

The L-threonate product grams are not elemental magnesium, and no conversion is supplied here. The bisglycinate study explicitly reported elemental magnesium. Study protocols are not personal instructions. NIH ODS lists an adult upper limit of 350 mg/day from supplements and medications, excluding food; it is not a target.

Bottom line

L-threonate has placebo-controlled sleep and cognition findings with important negative and mixed results. Bisglycinate has a small sleep-symptom finding. Neither fills the missing direct comparison.
