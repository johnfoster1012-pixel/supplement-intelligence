L-arginine

Evidence posture

Little evidence that oral arginine meaningfully improves exercise performance in healthy active adults.

What the evidence supports

NIH ODS describes limited and conflicting clinical trials and concludes that arginine generally has little to no effect on strength, endurance, nitric-oxide measures, blood flow, or recovery in healthy active adults.

Studied context

Studies have used a wide range, roughly 2–20 g/day. Oral arginine is less efficient than citrulline at raising blood arginine because of intestinal and hepatic metabolism.

Safety context

Short-term use up to about 9 g/day has generally been tolerated in studies; higher doses more often cause gastrointestinal symptoms and may lower blood pressure.

Product directness

V-NITRO lists L-arginine but does not publish its amount. The presence of arginine therefore does not support a nitric-oxide, pump, strength, endurance, or recovery claim for the finished product.
