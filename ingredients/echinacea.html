Echinacea

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Possible small effect on cold incidence for some preparations; evidence for shortening or treating colds remains uncertain and product-specific.

What the evidence supports

The 2014 Cochrane review found that echinacea products had not been shown to provide clear treatment benefit for colds, though a weak preventive effect from some preparations remained possible. Species, plant parts, and extraction methods vary widely, limiting generalization across products.

Studied context

Clinical trials use different Echinacea species and preparations. Without species, plant part, extract, and dose information, direct comparison to the research is weak.

Safety context

Short-term use is likely safe for many adults, but gastrointestinal effects and allergic reactions can occur. Drug interactions are possible, and evidence in children, pregnancy, and breastfeeding is limited.

Common questions

Does echinacea prevent colds?

Not clearly. A 2014 Cochrane review of 24 double-blind trials (4,631 people) found that none of the 12 prevention comparisons showed a significant difference on its own. Pooling them after the fact suggested a 10% to 20% lower relative risk of catching a cold, which the authors called of questionable clinical relevance (grade B).

Does echinacea shorten a cold?

The trials don't show it. Of 7 treatment trials in the same review that reported how long colds lasted, only one found a significant benefit over placebo (grade B).

Is echinacea safe?

It's likely safe for most adults for short periods, according to the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH). The most common side effects are digestive, such as stomach pain or nausea, and some people have allergic reactions, which may be severe. NCCIH says the evidence on interactions with drugs processed by the liver is conflicting, and that echinacea might interact with immunosuppressants or caffeine.

Review-level evidence highlights

Echinacea for preventing and treating the common cold (Cochrane systematic review; PMID 24554461)

Population/scope: 24 double-blind trials; 4,631 participants

Evidence scope: Prevention and treatment of the common cold

Finding: No clear treatment benefit was established; a weak preventive effect from some preparations remained possible.

Limitations: Strong clinical heterogeneity across species, plant parts, extracts, and trial methods prevented broad pooling and product-level generalization.

Directness: Relevant human evidence · verified 2026-10-02

Important: Ingredient-level evidence is not proof that a finished product has the same effect. Dose, form, population, duration, and formulation must match.
