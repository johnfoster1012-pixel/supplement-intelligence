Caffeine

Evidence posture

Good evidence for some endurance and high-intensity intermittent performance outcomes; effects vary by dose, habitual use, timing, and individual response.

What the evidence supports

NIH ODS notes that caffeine can reduce perceived fatigue and help sustain exercise intensity, with the clearest support for endurance and some intermittent high-intensity activities. It is not a universal strength or power enhancer.

Studied context

Sports-performance guidance commonly discusses about 2–6 mg/kg taken before exercise. Lower amounts can work in some people, and higher doses generally increase adverse effects without reliably improving performance further.

Safety context

Caffeine can cause insomnia, anxiety, jitteriness, gastrointestinal symptoms, and increased heart rate. Total intake from all sources matters, especially in products containing coffee, guarana, green tea, or yerba mate.

Product directness

Several current products contain coffee or other caffeine sources, but manufacturer pages generally do not publish total caffeine content. Trial-equivalent performance or safety claims therefore cannot be assigned to those finished products.
