Caffeine

Evidence posture

Strong evidence for acute ergogenic effects in endurance and some high-intensity/resistance-performance outcomes; effects depend on dose, timing, habitual use, and individual sensitivity.

What the evidence supports

NIH ODS describes numerous clinical trials with mostly consistent findings that caffeine can improve endurance-type and intermittent long-duration performance when taken before activity. Recent meta-analyses also support improvements in aerobic time-trial performance and some resistance-exercise power outcomes.

Studied context

Common performance studies use roughly 2–6 mg/kg before exercise, often 15–60 minutes pre-exercise. Higher doses do not necessarily improve results and increase adverse effects.

Safety context

For healthy adults, total caffeine intake around 400 mg/day is generally considered a level not usually associated with dangerous effects, but insomnia, anxiety, restlessness, nausea, tachycardia, and arrhythmia can occur. Combining multiple caffeine sources increases risk.

Product directness

LATTEKAFFE, V-LOVKAFE, V-NEUROKAFE, V-NRGY, V-ORGANEX, and V-THERMOKAFE contain coffee, caffeine, guarana, or related caffeine sources. The current manufacturer pages do not publish total caffeine per serving for these products, so trial-equivalent dosing and performance effects cannot be inferred.
