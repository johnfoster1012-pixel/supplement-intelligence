Milk thistle (Silybum marianum)

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Insufficient high-quality evidence for liver-disease benefits; broad liver-detox claims are not supported.

What the evidence supports

A 2002 review of randomized trials found no clear benefit for death rates, liver tissue, or liver blood tests. A 2008 review found fewer liver-related deaths in people with cirrhosis, but the trials were small and mixed. Evidence remains insufficient to support broad liver-treatment or detox claims.

Studied context

Milk-thistle extracts differ in silymarin content and composition. Evidence from a standardized extract should not be generalized to unspecified milk-thistle powder.

Safety context

Oral use is usually well tolerated, with digestive symptoms most common. Allergic reactions and supplement-quality problems can occur, and drug interactions are possible.

Common questions

What is milk thistle extract?

Milk thistle (Silybum marianum) is a tall plant with purple flowers, native to Europe. The main constituent of its extract is silymarin, a mixture of plant compounds, according to the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH). Products differ in how much silymarin they contain, and NCCIH notes that some have been found to contain amounts very different from their labels, or to be contaminated.

Is milk thistle a laxative?

No. It isn't sold or tested as one, and we found no trial of milk thistle on its own for constipation. The closest is a 2022 randomized crossover study of 51 people with constipation-type irritable bowel syndrome, which added a product combining silymarin with inulin (a fiber) and choline to a constipation diet. Stools per week and stool consistency did not differ significantly from the diet alone, and any effect couldn't be pinned on milk thistle (grade C). NCCIH lists bloating, nausea and gas as its most common side effects.

Does milk thistle help fatty liver?

The evidence is weak. A 2025 Cochrane review of 17 randomized trials in 2,069 adults with fatty liver disease (now called MASLD) found that silymarin alone may lower the liver enzyme ALT by about 7 U/L compared with placebo or no treatment (6 trials, 409 people), but the certainty was very low. Silymarin combined with other agents showed no clear change in liver enzymes, and no trial reported deaths or quality of life. Liver enzymes are a lab marker, not proof of a healthier liver, and the reviewers called the benefits and harms unclear (grade B).

Product directness

V-ORGANEX and V-TEDETOX list milk thistle, but the current manufacturer pages do not publish silymarin standardization or dose. Liver-support efficacy remains unverified.

Review-level evidence highlights

Milk thistle for the treatment of liver disease: a systematic review and meta-analysis (Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized placebo-controlled trials; PMID 12427501)

Population/scope: 14 trials in people with liver disease

Evidence scope: Mortality, liver histology, and biochemical liver outcomes

Finding: Milk thistle did not show reliable improvements in mortality, histology, or clinically meaningful liver-function outcomes.

Limitations: Trials were heterogeneous and often small; evidence was insufficient to exclude benefit or harm for specific preparations or subgroups.

Directness: Relevant human evidence · verified 2026-10-02

Important: Ingredient-level evidence is not proof that a finished product has the same effect. Dose, form, population, duration, and formulation must match.
