Milk thistle (silymarin)

Evidence posture

Insufficient high-quality evidence to conclude that milk thistle treats liver disease; human findings are inconsistent.

What the evidence supports

NCCIH states there is not enough high-quality evidence to reach definite conclusions about milk thistle. Trials in multiple liver diseases have been conflicting or too limited, including NCCIH-funded studies that did not show benefit for hepatitis C or NASH.

Studied context

Milk-thistle extracts vary substantially in silymarin composition, standardization, and product quality. Results from one extract should not be generalized to all milk-thistle ingredients.

Safety context

Oral milk thistle is generally well tolerated, with gastrointestinal effects most common. Allergic reactions can occur, and NCCIH notes quality concerns including mislabeled silymarin content and contamination in some products.

Product directness

V-ORGANEX and V-TEDETOX list milk thistle, but current manufacturer pages do not provide a standardized silymarin amount. Liver or detoxification efficacy should not be inferred from inclusion.
