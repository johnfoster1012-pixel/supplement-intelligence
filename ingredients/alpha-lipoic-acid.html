Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA)

Evidence posture

Evidence does not support ALA as a general blood-sugar/lipid treatment; diabetic-neuropathy pain evidence is mixed.

What the evidence supports

NCCIH reports that a 2019 review found ALA no better than placebo for blood sugar, cholesterol, or triglycerides in type 2 diabetes. Reviews of diabetic neuropathy have produced inconsistent results, though some analyses suggest pain reduction.

Studied context

Most clinically relevant ALA trials involve people with diabetes or diabetic neuropathy. Those findings should not be generalized to healthy users or broad 'metabolic support' claims.

Safety context

Large reviews generally find ALA reasonably tolerated; common side effects include headache, heartburn, nausea, and vomiting.

Product directness

NOURISH PLUS, S-BALANCE, and VITALPRO list ALA, but current manufacturer pages do not publish ALA dose. Finished-product glucose, neuropathy, or metabolic claims remain unverified.
