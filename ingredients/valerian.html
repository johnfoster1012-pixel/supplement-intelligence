Valerian (Valeriana officinalis)

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Evidence for sleep problems is inconsistent; authoritative guidance does not support presenting valerian as an established insomnia treatment.

What the evidence supports

A 2024 umbrella review found no convincing evidence that valerian treats insomnia, although some reviews report subjective sleep-quality improvement. Objective sleep outcomes, preparations, and study quality remain inconsistent.

Studied context

Short-term studies commonly use valerian root preparations at roughly 300–600 mg/day, but extracts and preparations vary.

Safety context

Short-term use appears generally tolerated in many adults, but long-term safety is unknown. Sedative effects, withdrawal after chronic use, and rare liver injury reports warrant caution.

Common questions

Does valerian work for insomnia?

The evidence says no. A 2024 umbrella review of 8 systematic reviews found no evidence that valerian treats insomnia. Some people reported better sleep quality, but that didn't show up on objective sleep measures (grade B). In 2017 the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommended against valerian for chronic insomnia in adults, according to NCCIH.

Is valerian safe?

It appears safe for short-term use by most adults, but long-term safety is unknown, according to the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH). Reported side effects include headache, stomach upset, mental dullness, excitability, uneasiness and vivid dreams. Withdrawal symptoms have been reported when people stop suddenly after long-term use, and liver injury has been reported in very rare cases, mostly with other herbs.

Can you take valerian with alcohol or sleeping pills?

NCCIH says not to. Because valerian might make you sleepy, it should not be taken with alcohol or sedatives. NCCIH also advises talking with your health care provider before using it with any medicine, and says little is known about its safety in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Review-level evidence highlights

Does valerian work for insomnia? An umbrella review of the evidence (Umbrella review of systematic reviews; PMID 38359657)

Population/scope: 8 eligible systematic reviews of valerian for sleep disturbances/insomnia

Evidence scope: Insomnia and sleep quality

Finding: The review found no convincing evidence that valerian treats insomnia, though subjective sleep-quality improvement was reported in some analyses.

Limitations: Existing studies are heterogeneous, often low quality, and use variable valerian preparations; objective sleep measures are not consistently improved.
