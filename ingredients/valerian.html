Valerian

Evidence posture

Evidence for insomnia is inconsistent; authoritative guidance does not support valerian as an established treatment for chronic insomnia.

What the evidence supports

NCCIH states there is not enough evidence to determine whether valerian is useful for any health condition. Sleep trials have inconsistent results, and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommended against valerian for chronic insomnia in adults.

Studied context

Short-term studies have often used about 300–600 mg/day, but preparations vary and the mechanism is not well standardized.

Safety context

Short-term use appears generally tolerated for most adults, but long-term safety is unknown. Sedation, headache, stomach upset, vivid dreams, and rare liver injury have been reported. Valerian should not be combined casually with alcohol or sedatives.

Product directness

V-ITALAY lists valerian but does not publish dose or extract standardization. A general 'sleep support' claim should therefore remain under review.
