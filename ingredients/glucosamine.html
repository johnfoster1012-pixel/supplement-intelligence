Glucosamine

Evidence posture

Knee-osteoarthritis evidence is inconsistent and formulation-specific; major professional guidelines disagree.

What the evidence supports

NCCIH concludes that it remains uncertain whether glucosamine helps knee osteoarthritis symptoms. Some studies and guidelines report possible benefit for particular prescription crystalline glucosamine sulfate preparations, while U.S. rheumatology and OARSI guidelines have recommended against glucosamine because higher-quality evidence has not shown important benefit.

Studied context

Glucosamine sulfate and glucosamine hydrochloride should not be assumed equivalent. Product quality and pharmaceutical-grade preparations may materially affect results.

Safety context

Large osteoarthritis studies have not identified major safety problems, but glucosamine may affect blood glucose in some people and has been associated with increased bleeding risk in people using warfarin.

Product directness

V-ITADOL lists glucosamine, but its current manufacturer page does not identify the glucosamine salt or dose. Evidence from specific pharmaceutical-grade glucosamine sulfate cannot be assigned to the finished product.
