Black walnut hull benefits, side effects and evidence

Short answer. Black walnut hull is the green outer husk of the black walnut (Juglans nigra), sold as capsules, tinctures and teas, most often in "parasite cleanse" products with wormwood and clove. We found no human trial of black walnut hull for parasites or for any other use.

What exists is laboratory work on juglone, a compound in the hull that can kill microbes in a dish and is also toxic to human cells at similar concentrations. People with a walnut or tree-nut allergy and pregnant women should avoid it.

Evidence posture

No human trials of black walnut hull were found for parasites or any other use; support is limited to laboratory studies of juglone and traditional use.

What black walnut hull is

The black walnut tree is native to eastern North America. Its fruit has three parts: a green husk (the hull), a hard shell, and the nut. Supplements use the hull, which is normally discarded when the nuts are processed.

The hull contains phenolic compounds, including a yellow pigment called juglone. Preparations from black walnut have long been used as hair and skin dyes and applied to the skin for ringworm and other fungal, bacterial or viral infections.

The nut is a different matter. Research on eating walnuts is about the kernel, mostly of the English walnut, and says nothing about hull supplements.

What the evidence supports

Parasites: no human trials

We searched PubMed for human trials of black walnut and found none of the hull, alone or combined with wormwood and clove. The parasite claim rests on laboratory findings and tradition:

  • A review of juglone lists antimicrobial and antifungal actions among its laboratory effects.
  • In one laboratory study, juglone stopped the growth of an amoeba (Acanthamoeba) in a dish.
  • A study of black walnut husk extracts notes that their phenolic compounds show antioxidant and antimicrobial properties.

Killing an organism in a dish does not show that a capsule clears an infection in a person. The compound has to survive digestion, reach the parasite at a high enough level, and do so without harming the person taking it. None of that has been tested.

The one human trial is of the nut

In a 2011 trial, 36 people ate 30 g a day of English or black walnuts for 30 days. English walnuts improved blood fats. The effects of black walnuts "were dependent on the participant's sex," and the authors concluded that more research was needed before making any recommendation about black walnuts. That trial was about eating the nut, not taking hull extract.

Studied context

No clinical study has tested black walnut hull in people, so there is no studied dose, duration or preparation. Laboratory studies used purified juglone or husk extracts on cells and microbes.

Side effects and safety

Because no human trial exists, side effects have not been measured. What is known comes from laboratory work and case reports.

  • Juglone is toxic to cells. In a laboratory study, juglone reduced the survival of human skin cells at concentrations of 1 to 20 micromolar. The amoeba study above saw its effect at 3 to 5 micromolar, the same range.
  • Embryo toxicity in animals. Juglone impaired the development of early mouse embryos in a laboratory study.
  • Skin staining and irritation. Black walnut juice stains the skin, and dermatitis from it has been described in the medical literature.
  • Allergy. Walnuts are among the leading causes of allergic reactions to tree nuts, some of them life-threatening, and allergens have been identified in black walnut. Whether hull products carry them has not been established.
  • Unknowns. We found no entry for black walnut in the NIH's liver-injury or breastfeeding databases, and no studies of long-term use or drug interactions.

Who should avoid black walnut hull

  • Anyone with a walnut or tree-nut allergy.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women, given the embryo findings and the lack of human data.
  • Children. No safety data exist.

One note for animal owners: black walnut wood shavings are a recognized cause of laminitis, a serious hoof condition, in horses.

If you think you have parasites

Symptoms blamed on parasites, such as bloating, fatigue and stomach upset, have many causes. Intestinal parasites are diagnosed with laboratory tests and treated with medicines chosen for the specific organism. See a clinician if you have lasting diarrhea, blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, or symptoms after travel.

Common questions

What is black walnut hull used for?

It is sold mainly for parasite cleanses, and traditionally applied to the skin for fungal infections. Neither use has been tested in a human trial that we could find.

Does black walnut hull kill parasites?

Not shown in people. A compound in the hull, juglone, has stopped the growth of microbes in laboratory dishes, at concentrations that also harm human cells.

Do black walnut, wormwood and clove work together?

We found no human trial of that combination.

What are the side effects of black walnut hull?

They have not been measured in a trial. Known concerns are allergic reactions in people with walnut allergy, skin staining and irritation, and laboratory evidence that juglone is toxic to cells.

Is black walnut hull safe in pregnancy?

It should be avoided. There are no human data, and juglone harmed early mouse embryos in a laboratory study.

Can I take black walnut hull if I am allergic to walnuts?

No. Walnuts are a leading cause of serious tree-nut allergy, and it has not been established that hull products are free of the allergens.

Is black walnut hull the same as eating walnuts?

No. The hull is the outer green husk. Studies of walnuts as food are about the nut inside, mostly the English walnut.
