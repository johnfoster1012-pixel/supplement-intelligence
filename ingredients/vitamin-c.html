Vitamin C

Evidence posture

Essential nutrient with established physiological roles; routine supplementation does not prevent colds in the general population, though it may modestly shorten them.

What the evidence supports

NIH ODS states that vitamin C is required for collagen synthesis and other physiological functions. Regular supplementation generally does not reduce cold incidence in the general population, but may modestly shorten cold duration; taking vitamin C only after symptoms start does not appear beneficial.

Studied context

Common-cold evidence often examines regular intake of at least 200 mg/day, with larger effects in people exposed to extreme physical stress or with marginal vitamin C status.

Safety context

High oral doses can cause diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramps. The adult tolerable upper intake level is 2,000 mg/day, and high doses can worsen iron overload in people with hemochromatosis.

Product directness

Several current products list vitamin C, but most do not publish a dose that can be matched to specific trials. Ingredient presence supports a nutrient-content statement, not a finished-product cold, immune, collagen, or antioxidant efficacy claim.
