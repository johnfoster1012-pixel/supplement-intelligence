Does creatine make you gain weight? What the studies actually show

Short answer. Yes, creatine can raise scale weight, by about 1 kg (2 pounds) in the first week in one trial, and that early gain is mostly water. The studies reviewed here do not show fat gain. With weight training, a review of 35 trials found about 1.1 kg more lean mass than training alone. Not medical advice.

Does creatine make you gain weight?

It can raise scale weight, mostly early and mostly from water. In a 7-day study of soccer players, the creatine group gained about 2.3 L of body water and 1.0 kg of body weight, while the placebo group had no significant change. The trials reviewed here do not show that this early gain is fat. [PMID 26509366; PMID 12937471]

How much weight do you gain on creatine?

In the studies reviewed, early gains were about 1 kg (2 pounds). Soccer players gained 1.0 kg in 7 days. Over months of weight training, a review of 35 trials found about 1.1 kg more lean mass with creatine than without it. Without exercise, lean mass barely changed, 0.03 kg, and that was not statistically significant. [PMID 26509366; PMID 35986981]

These are group averages. The review's 95% confidence interval for the resistance-training gain was 0.56 to 1.65 kg. [PMID 35986981] Grade B for the 7-day gain (one small trial); Grade A for the pooled lean-mass gain with training.

Is creatine weight gain water or fat?

The early gain looks like water. Studies measured total body water rising along with body weight, and none of the trials reviewed here show that the early gain is fat. A body scan can count extra water as lean mass, so a quick rise in lean mass is not automatically new muscle either. [PMID 12937471; PMID 36960692]

In Brooks's study of female dancers, body water and the DXA lean-mass estimate rose together in the seven who took creatine. The authors said larger trials are needed to tell whether muscle itself increased. [PMID 36960692] Grade B.

Why does the scale go up so fast on creatine?

Because body water can rise within days. Powers and colleagues found that creatine raised total body water and body mass over 28 days, while the split between water inside and outside cells stayed the same. In the soccer study, body water rose 2.3 L in just 7 days. [PMID 12937471; PMID 26509366]

Powers studied 16 men and 16 women who did resistance training, using 25 g a day for 7 days and then 5 g a day for 21 days, versus placebo. These are the amounts studied, not instructions. [PMID 12937471] Grade B.

Does creatine build muscle, or is it all water?

With weight training, creatine adds some lean mass. A review of 35 randomized trials found 1.10 kg more lean mass with creatine plus resistance training. But in one 13-week trial, the creatine group's extra lean mass appeared in the first 7 days, and then both groups gained the same 2 kg from training. [PMID 35986981; PMID 40292479]

Desai and colleagues randomized 63 adults to 5 g a day of creatine or a control group that took nothing. After the 7-day wash-in, the creatine group had 0.51 ± 1.79 kg more lean mass. Over the next 12 weeks of training both groups gained 2 kg, with no difference between them. [PMID 40292479] Grade B.

Do women gain weight on creatine?

The evidence is thinner for women. The 35-trial review found a 1.46 kg lean-mass gain in men but only 0.29 kg in women, and the women's result was not statistically significant. A small study in female dancers did find higher body water and lean-mass estimates on creatine. [PMID 35986981; PMID 36960692]

Review confidence intervals: men 0.47 to 2.46 kg; women −0.43 to 1.01 kg. [PMID 35986981] Grade B (subgroup results).

What amounts of creatine did these studies use?

Powers used 25 g a day for 7 days, then 5 g a day. Deminice used 0.3 g per kilogram a day for 7 days. Desai used 5 g a day for 13 weeks, and Brooks used 0.1 g per kilogram a day. These are research protocols, not personal instructions. [PMID 12937471; PMID 26509366; PMID 40292479; PMID 36960692]

How should I read the evidence grades?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or surrogate-only (a lab or scan measure instead of how people feel or function).
  • Grade C: animal, cell, mechanistic or anecdotal evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence.

We grade the specific claim, not the compound. One Phase 3 trial, or a surrogate outcome alone, is Grade B.
