Creatine HCl vs monohydrate: what studies actually compare

Short answer. Human head-to-head evidence for creatine HCl versus monohydrate is small and mixed, so a winner is not established. Claims that HCl works at a much lower amount or causes less bloating are not settled by these trials. Monohydrate has a much larger overall research base, which is a separate point from winning a direct comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Is creatine HCl better than monohydrate?
That is not established. An 8-week resistance-training trial found both forms beat placebo for strength and muscle-size measures, with no benefit of HCl over monohydrate. [PMID 39545789]

Did any study find HCl was better?
A 4-week soccer study reported greater short-term physical-performance and peak-power changes with ethyl ester and HCl than with monohydrate. That trial was small (10 people per group) and short, and it conflicts with the 8-week training comparison. [PMID 42720250]

Does HCl work at a lower amount?
Claims that HCl works at a much lower amount are not settled here. The 8-week trial used 0.03 g/kg of HCl, and compared it with monohydrate either loaded or used at 0.03 g/kg. Both monohydrate approaches and HCl outperformed placebo, without an HCl advantage. [PMID 39545789]

Does HCl cause less bloating?
The evidence reviewed does not include a clear head-to-head bloating or stomach-tolerance result that establishes HCl as better tolerated.

Are there many independent comparisons?
No. Human head-to-head evidence remains limited to small, short trials, and the two main comparisons do not agree.

Which form has more research overall?
Creatine monohydrate has a much larger research base, including lean-mass reviews. A larger monohydrate evidence base is not the same as a head-to-head win. [PMID 35986981]

How to read the evidence

  • Grade A: a published Phase 3 randomized trial, or for nutrient pages a published systematic review of randomized trials, for outcomes reported within that evidence.
  • Grade B: a Phase 2 trial, exploratory or subgroup analysis, small human trial, observational study, or labeling statement beyond a trial abstract.
  • Grade C: uncontrolled reports, animal or laboratory inference, mechanism-based predictions, and comparisons between separate trials.

The eight-week resistance-training comparison

Eghbali, Arazi, and Suzuki randomized 40 participants aged 18–25 into four groups of 10. The study used HCl at 0.03 g/kg, monohydrate with a loading period followed by 0.03 g/kg, monohydrate at 0.03 g/kg without loading, or placebo, alongside eight weeks of resistance training. These are study protocols, not instructions. Grade B.

Supplementation groups improved strength, arm and thigh muscle cross-sectional area, skeletal muscle mass, and percent body fat compared with placebo. The authors found no benefit of HCl over monohydrate.

The four-week soccer comparison found a different result

Duan and colleagues randomized 40 collegiate male soccer players into monohydrate, ethyl ester, HCl, or placebo groups, with 10 per group, during four weeks of repeated-sprint training. Grade B.

All training groups improved. Creatine groups had greater changes than placebo, and ethyl ester and HCl showed greater physical-performance and peak-power gains than monohydrate. There were no significant group differences for mean power or VO2max.

The small groups, short duration, male soccer population, and different outcomes limit interpretation. This conflicts with the eight-week comparison and does not establish broader superiority.

What remains untested here

The comparisons do not provide a clear head-to-head bloating or stomach-tolerance result. A claim of better tolerance needs its own evidence. A 35-study, 1,192-participant lean-mass review gives context but does not establish HCl-versus-monohydrate superiority.

Bottom line

Both direct comparisons are small and short, and their findings differ. Monohydrate has more research overall, but these trials do not settle a universal winner, a lower-amount HCl advantage, or a bloating advantage.
