Senna

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Established stimulant-laxative activity with supportive constipation evidence; this does not support broad detox or weight-loss claims.

What the evidence supports

Senna is a stimulant laxative with human evidence supporting increased bowel movements and symptom improvement in constipation. That pharmacologic laxative effect is distinct from claims of systemic detoxification, fat loss, or generalized cleansing.

Studied context

Senna products vary in sennoside content and dosing. Short-term constipation use is distinct from prolonged use or multi-herb detox regimens.

Safety context

Senna is generally tolerated when used as recommended for short periods, but high doses or prolonged use can cause adverse effects and has been associated with clinically apparent liver injury.

Product directness

V-TEDETOX contains senna leaf and senna extract, but the current manufacturer page does not publish sennoside content or dose. The site should not imply a predictable laxative effect or a detox benefit from ingredient presence alone.

Review-level evidence highlights

Efficacy and Safety of Over-the-Counter Therapies for Chronic Constipation: An Updated Systematic Review (Systematic review of randomized controlled trials; PMID 33767108)

Population/scope: 41 randomized trials of over-the-counter constipation therapies in adults

Evidence scope: Chronic constipation efficacy and safety

Finding: The review judged evidence for senna as good and supportive of use as a laxative for chronic constipation.

Limitations: The review covered multiple therapies and trial designs; this supports laxative activity, not detoxification, weight loss, or systemic-cleansing claims.

Directness: Relevant human evidence · verified 2026-10-02

Important: Ingredient-level evidence is not proof that a finished product has the same effect. Dose, form, population, duration, and formulation must match.
