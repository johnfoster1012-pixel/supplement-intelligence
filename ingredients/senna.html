Senna

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Established stimulant-laxative activity with supportive constipation evidence; this does not support broad detox or weight-loss claims.

What the evidence supports

Senna is a stimulant laxative with human evidence supporting increased bowel movements and symptom improvement in constipation. That pharmacologic laxative effect is distinct from claims of systemic detoxification, fat loss, or generalized cleansing.

Studied context

Senna products vary in sennoside content and dosing. Short-term constipation use is distinct from prolonged use or multi-herb detox regimens.

Safety context

Senna is generally tolerated when used as recommended for short periods, but high doses or prolonged use can cause adverse effects and has been associated with clinically apparent liver injury.

Common questions

How does senna work, and what is in it?

Senna's active compounds are sennosides. Bacteria in the colon turn them into rhein anthrone, the form that acts on the bowel, which makes senna a stimulant laxative rather than a fiber. Over-the-counter senna labels say it generally produces a bowel movement within 6 to 12 hours. In a small study of 10 healthy volunteers, run by a laxative maker, blood levels of rhein, a breakdown product, peaked twice: 3 to 5 hours and 10 to 11 hours after a dose.

Is senna good for constipation?

For chronic constipation, yes, on limited evidence. In a 2021 randomized trial of 90 adults (mostly women), 69.2% improved overall on senna for 28 days, against 68.3% on magnesium oxide and 11.7% on placebo, and no severe treatment-related side effects were reported. The 2023 American Gastroenterological Association and American College of Gastroenterology guideline gives senna a conditional recommendation for chronic idiopathic constipation; polyethylene glycol and sodium picosulfate get strong ones (grade B).

Is senna safe, and how long can you take it?

Over-the-counter senna labels say not to use laxatives for longer than one week unless directed by a doctor. They say to ask a doctor before use if you have stomach pain, nausea, vomiting, or a sudden change in bowel habits lasting more than two weeks, and to stop and ask a doctor if there is rectal bleeding or no bowel movement after use. The 28-day trial above reported no severe treatment-related side effects (grade B). High doses or prolonged use have been linked to liver injury, as the safety section above notes.

Review-level evidence highlights

Efficacy and Safety of Over-the-Counter Therapies for Chronic Constipation: An Updated Systematic Review (Systematic review of randomized controlled trials; PMID 33767108)

Population/scope: 41 randomized trials of over-the-counter constipation therapies in adults

Evidence scope: Chronic constipation efficacy and safety

Finding: The review judged evidence for senna as good and supportive of use as a laxative for chronic constipation.

Limitations: The review covered multiple therapies and trial designs; this supports laxative activity, not detoxification, weight loss, or systemic-cleansing claims.

Directness: Relevant human evidence · verified 2026-10-02
