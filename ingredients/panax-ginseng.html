Panax ginseng (Asian ginseng)

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Small or context-specific fatigue and cognitive effects are possible, but pooled results are inconsistent and broad adaptogen or performance claims are not established.

What the evidence supports

Systematic reviews report mixed results. Some analyses find small fatigue benefits in selected populations, while pooled overall fatigue and physical-performance outcomes are inconsistent. Cognitive findings are also outcome- and preparation-specific, so results should not be generalized across ginseng products.

Studied context

Ginseng preparations differ substantially in extraction method and ginsenoside content. Findings from one preparation should not be generalized to an unspecified ginseng extract.

Safety context

Short-term oral use appears generally tolerated for many adults, but insomnia is common. NCCIH notes possible drug interactions, blood-clotting concerns, blood-sugar effects, autoimmune concerns, and pregnancy cautions.

Common questions

Does ginseng help with fatigue?

Only a little, and not consistently. A 2023 review of 19 randomized trials found no significant drop in fatigue overall. Ginseng alone reduced general fatigue, and herbal formulas containing ginseng helped chronic fatigue, but the effects were small. Ginseng did not increase side effects compared with controls (grade B).

Can ginseng cause insomnia?

Yes. Trouble sleeping is Asian ginseng's most common side effect, according to the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH). Uncommon reported effects include severe rash, liver damage and severe allergic reactions. NCCIH also says ginseng may worsen autoimmune disorders and interfere with blood clotting, and that short-term use of up to 6 months appears safe for most people.

Does ginseng affect blood sugar?

It might lower it. NCCIH cites a 2022 review of 20 studies (1,295 people) in which Asian ginseng improved fasting blood glucose in people with prediabetes or diabetes, but says research on blood sugar control is inconclusive and conflicting overall (grade B). NCCIH advises people with diabetes to talk with their health care provider before using it, since ginseng might interact with some medicines.

Review-level evidence highlights

Ginseng and Ginseng Herbal Formulas for Symptomatic Management of Fatigue: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials; PMID 36730693)

Population/scope: 19 randomized clinical trials across fatigue populations

Evidence scope: Fatigue severity, quality of life, and adverse events

Finding: The pooled overall analysis did not show a significant fatigue reduction, although small benefits appeared in selected subgroups such as chronic or general fatigue.

Limitations: Effects were small and varied by ginseng type, fatigue condition, and assessment method; herbal formulas are not interchangeable with standardized Panax ginseng products.

Directness: Relevant human evidence · verified 2026-10-02

Important: Ingredient-level evidence is not proof that a finished product has the same effect. Dose, form, population, duration, and formulation must match.
