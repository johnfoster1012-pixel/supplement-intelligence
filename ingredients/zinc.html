Zinc

Evidence reviewed: 2026-10-02

Evidence posture

Essential nutrient with established deficiency-related roles; cold benefits are formulation- and timing-specific rather than a generic property of zinc-containing supplements.

What the evidence supports

Zinc is an essential nutrient. A 2024 Cochrane review found little or no effect on preventing colds but suggested zinc may shorten the duration of an ongoing cold, with more non-serious adverse events and substantial variation across formulations and study methods.

Studied context

Cold studies generally use zinc acetate or gluconate lozenges/syrups at substantially higher short-term intakes than typical multivitamin doses. Those results should not be generalized to ordinary oral powders or capsules.

Safety context

Excess zinc can cause nausea and other adverse effects and can interfere with copper status and some medications. Dose and duration matter.

Product directness

D-FENZ KIDS, NOURISH+, V-DAILY, and V-DAILY SACHET list zinc, but their use forms and amounts are not equivalent to studied cold lozenges. A cold-treatment claim would be inappropriate.

Review-level evidence highlights

Zinc for prevention and treatment of the common cold (Cochrane systematic review; PMID 38719213)

Population/scope: Randomized studies of zinc for common-cold prevention or treatment

Evidence scope: Cold incidence, duration, symptoms, and adverse events

Finding: Zinc may have little or no effect on preventing colds but may shorten the duration of an ongoing cold.

Limitations: Interventions, formulations, doses, co-therapies, and outcomes varied widely; non-serious adverse events were more frequent with zinc.

Directness: Relevant human evidence · verified 2026-10-02

Important: Ingredient-level evidence is not proof that a finished product has the same effect. Dose, form, population, duration, and formulation must match.
