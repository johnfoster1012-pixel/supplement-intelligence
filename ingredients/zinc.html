Zinc

Evidence posture

Essential nutrient with established deficiency-related roles; cold benefits are formulation- and timing-specific rather than a generic property of zinc-containing supplements.

What the evidence supports

NIH ODS notes that zinc is essential for immune function, growth, protein/DNA synthesis, and wound healing. For the common cold, some lozenge or syrup studies suggest shorter symptom duration when started early, but evidence certainty is low and severity effects are inconsistent.

Studied context

Cold studies generally use zinc acetate or gluconate lozenges/syrups at substantially higher short-term intakes than typical multivitamin doses. Those results should not be generalized to ordinary oral powders or capsules.

Safety context

Excess zinc can cause nausea and other adverse effects and can interfere with copper status and some medications. Dose and duration matter.

Product directness

D-FENZ KIDS, NOURISH+, V-DAILY, and V-DAILY SACHET list zinc, but their use forms and amounts are not equivalent to studied cold lozenges. A cold-treatment claim would be inappropriate.
