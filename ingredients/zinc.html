Zinc

Evidence posture

Essential nutrient with established deficiency-related roles; cold-treatment effects are formulation- and dose-specific and should not be generalized to ordinary supplement blends.

What the evidence supports

NIH ODS describes zinc as essential for enzyme activity, immune function, protein/DNA synthesis, wound healing, growth, and cell signaling. Meta-analyses of oral zinc for acute respiratory infections/common colds suggest that some formulations, particularly lozenges, may shorten illness duration, but results are heterogeneous and depend on formulation and dose.

Studied context

Cold-treatment trials often use lozenges and elemental-zinc exposures very different from multivitamin or powder formulations. Nutritional adequacy is distinct from therapeutic-dose cold treatment.

Safety context

The adult tolerable upper intake level is 40 mg/day. Chronic intakes around 50 mg/day or more can impair copper absorption and may reduce immune function and HDL cholesterol; nausea and gastrointestinal effects also occur.

Product directness

D-FENZ KIDS, NOURISH PLUS, V-DAILY, and V-DAILY SACHET list zinc but are not zinc-lozenge cold-treatment products, and current pages do not consistently publish zinc dose. Cold-duration findings should not be assigned to these products.
