Resveratrol

Evidence posture

Human evidence is heterogeneous and does not establish an anti-aging or longevity benefit; some cardiometabolic biomarkers may improve in selected populations.

What the evidence supports

Meta-analyses of randomized trials report modest improvements in some cardiometabolic markers in people with metabolic syndrome, but results vary by population and protocol. A 2025 meta-analysis found no significant overall effect of resveratrol on human SIRT1 gene expression, protein expression, or serum levels, undermining simplistic 'SIRT1 activation equals anti-aging' claims.

Studied context

Human trials vary greatly in dose, formulation, duration, health status, and outcome. Biomarker changes are not equivalent to demonstrated reductions in disease events or slower aging.

Safety context

Human trials generally report reasonable tolerability, but gastrointestinal effects increase at higher doses and long-term efficacy/safety remain incompletely established. Reviews note poor bioavailability and substantial protocol-dependent variability.

Product directness

COLLAGEN COMPLEX and NOURISH PLUS list resveratrol without a published dose. The current evidence does not justify assigning anti-aging, longevity, or cardiometabolic treatment effects to these finished products.
