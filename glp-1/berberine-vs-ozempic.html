Berberine vs Ozempic: what the trials show

Short answer. Berberine is not a natural version of Ozempic. The two have never been compared head to head, and in their separate trials the effects are far apart: pooled berberine trials show about 1 to 2 kg of weight loss, while semaglutide at its weight-management dose produced an average loss of about 15% of body weight.

Berberine does have real, modest evidence for lowering blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes. That is a different claim from replacing a prescription drug, and nobody should stop a prescribed medication in favor of a supplement without their clinician.

They are different kinds of things

Ozempic is a brand of semaglutide, a prescription GLP-1 receptor agonist injected once a week. It is FDA-approved to improve blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes and to lower certain cardiovascular and kidney risks in that group. Wegovy is the semaglutide brand approved for weight management, at a higher dose.

Berberine is a plant alkaloid sold as a dietary supplement. It does not act on the GLP-1 receptor the way semaglutide does. Supplements are not FDA-approved to treat any condition and do not have to prove they work before being sold, so dose and purity vary between products.

The "nature's Ozempic" label comes from social media and marketing, not from a study comparing the two. We searched PubMed for a randomized trial of berberine against, or alongside, semaglutide, liraglutide or tirzepatide and found none.

Weight loss

Two meta-analyses of randomized trials have pooled berberine's effect on weight. A 2020 analysis of 12 trials found an average of 2.07 kg more weight lost than control. A larger 2026 analysis of 23 trials found a smaller figure, 0.88 kg. The authors of the 2026 analysis asked for better reporting of the purity and dose of the berberine used and noted common weaknesses in blinding and randomization.

In STEP 1, 1,961 adults with overweight or obesity and without diabetes took semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks alongside lifestyle support. Average weight change was 14.9% with semaglutide against 2.4% with placebo; about half of the semaglutide group lost 15% or more.

The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health describes the evidence for berberine and weight loss as not conclusive.

Blood sugar

This is where berberine's evidence is strongest. A meta-analysis of 37 randomized trials in 3,048 people with type 2 diabetes found berberine lowered HbA1c by about 0.63 percentage points and fasting glucose by about 0.82 mmol/L compared with control. The size of the effect depended on how high blood sugar was at the start. In a small 2008 pilot study of 36 adults with newly diagnosed type 2 diabetes, berberine's effect on blood sugar over three months was similar to metformin's.

The limits are important. The trials used different formulations and doses, and the pooled analysis drew on both English and Chinese-language databases, so study quality varies. By comparison, in the 30-week monotherapy trial on the Ozempic label, HbA1c fell by 1.4 to 1.6 percentage points against 0.1 with placebo.

Safety

Berberine. Stomach and bowel side effects are the most common; in the 2008 pilot study about a third of participants had temporary gastrointestinal effects. In the pooled diabetes trials berberine did not raise the overall rate of side effects or of low blood sugar. It can interact with medicines, and NCCIH advises against use during pregnancy or breastfeeding and in infants. More detail is on our berberine evidence page.

Semaglutide. Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting and constipation are common. The label carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents, whose relevance to humans is unknown, and warnings that include low blood sugar when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.

Together. Taking berberine with a GLP-1 drug has not been studied. Both lower blood sugar, so talk to your prescriber first, particularly if you take other diabetes medication.

Common questions

Can berberine replace Ozempic?

No evidence supports that. The two have not been compared in a trial, and the effects seen in separate trials are much smaller for berberine. Do not stop a prescribed medication without your clinician.

Does berberine work like a GLP-1 drug?

No. Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Berberine is a plant compound that does not act on that receptor in the same way.

How much weight do people lose on berberine?

Pooled trials show roughly 1 to 2 kg more than control, and the quality of those trials is mixed.

Is berberine useful for blood sugar?

Pooled trials in people with type 2 diabetes show a modest reduction in HbA1c. It is not an approved diabetes treatment, and products vary in dose and purity.
