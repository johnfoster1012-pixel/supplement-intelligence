Eloralintide: what the trials show for weight loss, side effects and how it works

Short answer. Eloralintide (LY3841136) is an experimental once-weekly injection from Eli Lilly that acts on amylin receptors, not GLP-1 receptors. In a 48-week Phase 2 trial of 263 adults with obesity or overweight, average weight change was −9% to −20% across the doses studied, versus −0.4% on placebo. Nausea and fatigue were the most common side effects. It is not FDA-approved, and one Phase 2 trial is good evidence, one trial (grade B). Not medical advice.

What is eloralintide?

Eloralintide (code name LY3841136) is an experimental weight-loss drug from Eli Lilly. It is a long-acting, once-weekly injection that activates amylin receptors, the same receptors the body's own hormone amylin uses. It is not a GLP-1 drug. It is in Phase 3 trials and is not FDA-approved. [Phase 2 trial; Phase 1 multiple-dose trial]

The published human studies so far are one Phase 2 trial in 263 adults and two smaller Phase 1 studies (100 and 48 people). All three were funded by Lilly, and most authors were Lilly employees. [Phase 2 trial; Phase 1 multiple-dose trial; Phase 1 single-dose study] Lilly also tests it combined with tirzepatide, under the name EloraTZP (see below).

How does eloralintide work?

Amylin is a hormone the pancreas releases with insulin after meals. It slows stomach emptying, lowers glucagon and signals fullness to the brain. Eloralintide copies that signal and lasts long enough for weekly shots. In lab cells it favored one amylin receptor type over related receptors. [Fischer review; Phase 1 single-dose study]

In cell tests, eloralintide activated the human amylin 1 receptor about 12 times more strongly than the calcitonin receptor and 11 times more than the amylin 3 receptor. In rats it lowered food intake and body weight, mostly through fat loss, and caused less food aversion than cagrilintide, a less selective amylin drug. [Phase 1 single-dose study] These are cell and animal results. Grade C for any claim that this selectivity means fewer side effects in people.

Amylin drugs are not new. Pramlintide was the first approved amylin analog, but it needed frequent injections and its weight effect was modest. [Alhazmi review] Eloralintide is one of several newer long-acting versions. [Bailey review]

How much weight did people lose on eloralintide?

In the 48-week Phase 2 trial, average weight change ranged from −9% to −20% across the eloralintide groups, versus −0.4% on placebo. The 263 adults had obesity or overweight and did not have type 2 diabetes. It is one trial, so this is good evidence, one trial (grade B). [Phase 2 trial]

Who was in it: 263 adults at 46 US centers, average age 49, average weight 109.1 kg (about 240 lb) and average BMI 39.1. 78% were women and 78% were White. The trial ran from February 2024 to August 2025 and is registered as NCT06230523. Grade B for weight loss versus placebo.

What did the earlier Phase 1 trials show?

Smaller, shorter results pointed the same way. In a 12-week study of 100 people with obesity or overweight, average weight loss ranged from 2.6% to 11.3% across dose groups. In 48 healthy people, one shot of 4 mg or 12 mg was followed by −2.5% and −4.4% at week 4, versus +0.6% on placebo. [Phase 1 multiple-dose trial; Phase 1 single-dose study]

The 12-week study ran at three US centers. Its participants averaged 44 years old with a BMI of 32.6, and 29% were women. Each cohort started at its full dose with no step-up. [Phase 1 multiple-dose trial] The single-dose study tested doses from 0.04 mg to 12 mg in healthy people with an average BMI of 27.5 (NCT05295940). [Phase 1 single-dose study] Short, small studies: Grade B.

What are the side effects of eloralintide?

Nausea and fatigue were the most common side effects in the Phase 2 trial. Nausea affected 11% to 64% of people across eloralintide groups, versus 14% on placebo. Fatigue affected 0% to 46%, versus 12% on placebo. Long-term safety is unknown. [Phase 2 trial]

Rates did not rise neatly with dose (64% nausea at 6 mg, 33% at 9 mg), and each group had only 24 to 54 people. The abstract does not explain the pattern and does not report how many people stopped because of side effects. [Phase 2 trial]

In the 12-week Phase 1 study, the most common side effects were lower appetite (19%), headache (12%), fatigue (11%) and COVID-19 (11%). Diarrhea (10%), nausea (8%) and vomiting (4%) were less common. Most were mild. There were no deaths, and the one serious adverse event was judged unrelated to the drug. [Phase 1 multiple-dose trial] In the single-dose study, 9 people on eloralintide reported 16 side effects, 15 of them mild. [Phase 1 single-dose study]

A 2026 review noted that eloralintide's side-effect pattern looked different from GLP-1 drugs, with fatigue, headache and appetite loss standing out. [Panou review] Another review says nausea with amylin drugs shows up mostly while the dose is being raised and usually fades. That is a statement about the drug class, not an eloralintide trial result. [Bailey review] Grade B for the Phase 2 rates.

Does eloralintide make you tired?

Fatigue was common in the Phase 2 trial: 21% to 46% in the groups that reached 6 mg or 9 mg, versus 12% on placebo. At 1 mg it was 0%, and at 3 mg 13%. No trial has shown why. Reviews flag fatigue as a feature that sets eloralintide apart from GLP-1 drugs. [Phase 2 trial; Panou review]

The abstract reports how many people had fatigue, not how bad it was or how long it lasted. Grade B for the rates; the cause is unknown.

Is eloralintide better than tirzepatide?

Nobody knows yet. No published trial has compared eloralintide alone with tirzepatide. A 2026 network meta-analysis ranked high-dose eloralintide above semaglutide 2.4 mg by indirect comparison, but its authors called the data sparse, low-certainty and preliminary. Cross-trial isn't head-to-head. [network meta-analysis]

That analysis pooled six randomized trials (4,642 people, 12 to 68 weeks) of newer amylin drugs and estimated weight change versus placebo: −18.01% for high-dose eloralintide and −11.45% for semaglutide 2.4 mg. The abstract does not report a tirzepatide comparison. [network meta-analysis] Grade C for any claim that one drug beats another, because no trial put them side by side.

EloraTZP (eloralintide plus tirzepatide). Lilly is also testing the two drugs together. A Phase 2 trial in 367 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes (NCT06603571) finished in July 2026. Lilly announced results on September 30, 2026, and presented them at the EASD meeting. [Lilly release] We found no peer-reviewed paper as of November 26, 2026, so this page does not quote its numbers. Two Phase 3 EloraTZP trials are registered: AMPLIFY-1 (NCT07881432) and AMPLIFY-2 (NCT07881458). For tirzepatide's own trial data, see tirzepatide side effects and tirzepatide vs semaglutide.

Eloralintide vs retatrutide: how do they differ?

They act on different hormones. Eloralintide targets amylin receptors. Retatrutide is a triple agonist that targets three others: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon. Both are experimental Lilly drugs, and neither is approved. They have never been tested against each other, so any comparison of their weight results is between separate trials. [Lempesis review; Phase 2 trial; our retatrutide page]

The trials also differ in length, people and analysis. The trials differ in length, in the people enrolled and in how results were analyzed. Grade C for any claim that one causes more weight loss. For retatrutide's own data, see what retatrutide does and retatrutide side effects.

Is eloralintide a GLP-1 drug?

No. Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) works on the GLP-1 receptor, and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) on GIP and GLP-1 receptors. Eloralintide works on amylin receptors instead. Both kinds of drug reduce appetite, but through different signals. Reviews describe amylin drugs as a separate class in development. [Alhazmi review; Fischer review]

One review notes that early eloralintide studies reported a lower pulse rate, while GLP-1 drugs tend to raise heart rate. [Sigalov review] We could not check those numbers against a primary abstract, so we don't quote them. No amylin drug has finished a cardiovascular outcomes trial. Grade C for any heart-protection claim.

Is eloralintide FDA-approved, and when could it be available?

No. Eloralintide is investigational and not FDA-approved. Lilly is running Phase 3 trials called ENLIGHTEN. Their estimated primary completion dates on the registry start in January 2028. No approval date has been set. Anything sold online as eloralintide is not an approved, FDA-reviewed drug. Unapproved means unreviewed. [ClinicalTrials.gov]

What is still unknown about eloralintide?

A lot. There is one Phase 2 trial of 48 weeks. Nobody knows long-term safety, what happens to weight after stopping, or whether it lowers heart attacks or other hard outcomes. The Phase 2 trial excluded type 2 diabetes and enrolled mostly women. We found no journal paper yet on eloralintide alone in people with diabetes. [Phase 2 trial; Sigalov review]

All three human studies were funded by Lilly. We could not check human muscle-versus-fat data against a primary abstract, and the Phase 2 abstract does not report dropout rates or how side effects changed over time. Phase 2 is a signal, not a label. For what is known about stopping approved drugs, see stopping a GLP-1; for muscle, see GLP-1 drugs and muscle loss.

Eloralintide FAQ

Who makes eloralintide?

Eli Lilly, the company behind tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound). Its code name is LY3841136. [Phase 1 single-dose study]

How is eloralintide given in trials?

As a once-weekly injection under the skin. The doses studied in Phase 2 ranged from 1 mg to 9 mg. These are trial protocols, not dosing instructions. [Phase 2 trial]

Is eloralintide the same as cagrilintide?

No. Both are long-acting amylin drugs, but cagrilintide also acts on the calcitonin receptor, while eloralintide was built to favor the amylin 1 receptor. Cagrilintide is the amylin half of CagriSema. [Bailey review; Phase 1 single-dose study]

Is eloralintide a peptide?

Yes. It is a modified version of amylin, which is a peptide hormone. Reviews group it with other long-acting amylin peptides. [Bailey review] It is a drug in clinical trials, not a supplement. See peptide supplements vs peptide drugs.

How should I read the evidence grades?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or surrogate-only (a lab or scan measure instead of how people feel or function).
  • Grade C: animal, cell, mechanistic or anecdotal evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence.

We grade the specific claim, not the compound. A single Phase 2 trial is Grade B, even in a top journal.
