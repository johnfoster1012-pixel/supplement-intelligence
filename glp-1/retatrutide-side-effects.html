Retatrutide side effects: what the TRIUMPH trials show

Short answer. The most common retatrutide side effects in trials were nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting, and they rose with dose. Two less familiar ones, an altered skin sensation called dysesthesia and low blood pressure, were also more common than with placebo. Retatrutide is not approved, and long-term safety is unknown. Not medical advice.

What are the most common retatrutide side effects?

Stomach and bowel problems: nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting. In every published retatrutide trial they were the most common side effects, and they rose with dose. In the TRIUMPH-1 obesity trial, Lilly reported nausea in 28.6% to 42.4% of people on the studied doses, versus 14.8% on placebo. [Lilly release; TRIUMPH-2]

That stomach and bowel problems are the most common side effects, and more common at higher doses, is Grade A: every randomized retatrutide trial we read agrees. The exact rates below come from one trial each, so they are Grade B. The doses are the ones studied in the trials, not dosing instructions.

The published TRIUMPH-1 paper says stomach and bowel events were the most common but gives no percentages. The figures in this table come from Lilly's company release, not the peer-reviewed paper. [TRIUMPH-1 paper; Lilly release]

What side effects did the TRIUMPH-2 trial report?

TRIUMPH-2 tested retatrutide in 1,152 adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes for 80 weeks. Diarrhea affected 27% to 34% on the studied doses versus 13% on placebo, and nausea 14% to 28% versus 8%. Low blood pressure and dysesthesia were also more common than with placebo. These figures come from the published paper. [TRIUMPH-2]

What is dysesthesia on retatrutide?

Dysesthesia is an unusual or unpleasant skin sensation, such as tingling or burning. It appeared more often with retatrutide than with placebo: 5.1% to 12.5% versus 0.9% in TRIUMPH-1 (company data) and 4% to 7% versus 1% in TRIUMPH-2. Its cause is not established. [Lilly release; TRIUMPH-2]

Lilly said most cases were mild to moderate and resolved during treatment. [Lilly release] Grade B: one peer-reviewed trial and one company release.

Does retatrutide lower blood pressure too much?

It caused low blood pressure (hypotension) in some people. In TRIUMPH-2, hypotension was reported in 1%, 5% and 6% of people at the 4, 9 and 12 mg studied doses, versus under 1% on placebo. That was 4, 14 and 18 people against 1. Anyone already on blood pressure medicine should raise this with their prescriber. [TRIUMPH-2]

Grade B (one trial). The paper does not say how severe these episodes were.

How many people stopped retatrutide because of side effects?

Most people kept taking it. In TRIUMPH-1, Lilly reported that 4.1%, 6.9% and 11.3% stopped because of side effects at the 4, 9 and 12 mg studied doses, versus 4.9% on placebo. In TRIUMPH-2, 4%, 12% and 8% stopped because of side effects or death, versus 5%. [Lilly release; TRIUMPH-2]

In TRIUMPH-2, 965 of 1,152 people (84%) completed the study drug. [TRIUMPH-2] Grade B: the rates did not rise neatly with dose, and each comes from one trial.

Does starting at a lower dose reduce retatrutide side effects?

In Phase 2 it seemed to. Registry results for the obesity trial show nausea in 17.1% of the 8 mg group that started at 2 mg, versus 60.0% of the 8 mg group that started at 4 mg, and 11.4% on placebo. Each of those arms had 35 people. In TRIUMPH-1, everyone started at 2 mg. [trial registry; Lilly release]

The Phase 2 paper says stomach and bowel side effects were dose-related, mostly mild to moderate, and partly reduced by a lower starting dose. [Phase 2 obesity trial] Lilly reports that TRIUMPH-1 participants started at 2 mg once weekly and stepped up every four weeks to their assigned dose. [Lilly release] These describe the trial design, not dosing instructions. Grade B.

In the Phase 2 diabetes trial, mild-to-moderate stomach and bowel side effects were reported in 35% of people on retatrutide overall, from 13% at 0.5 mg to 50% in the 8 mg fast-escalation group, versus 13% on placebo and 35% on dulaglutide. There was no severe low blood sugar and there were no deaths. [Phase 2 diabetes trial] Grade B.

Does retatrutide raise heart rate?

In the Phase 2 obesity trial, heart rate rose with dose, peaked at 24 weeks and then declined. The sources we checked give no beats-per-minute figures. How much it rises, and whether that matters long term, is not established. [Phase 2 obesity trial]

Grade B for a dose-related rise (one Phase 2 trial). The size of the rise is unknown from the sources read.

Were serious side effects reported with retatrutide?

Yes, more than with placebo in some trials. The knee osteoarthritis paper, covering TRIUMPH-4 and part of TRIUMPH-1, reported more serious adverse events with retatrutide than placebo in both trials, without percentages in its abstract. No long-term safety or heart-outcome results have been published. [knee osteoarthritis paper]

These trials ran 68 to 80 weeks. Long-term safety outside trials, cardiovascular outcomes and routine-care use are not established. Grade B for the serious-event finding (one paper).

Are retatrutide side effects worse than tirzepatide side effects?

No trial has compared them directly yet; TRIUMPH-5 is designed to. In separate trials, nausea affected 28.6% to 42.4% on retatrutide in TRIUMPH-1 (company data) and 25% to 29% on tirzepatide in the Zepbound trials, but placebo rates, doses and lengths differed. So any comparison is Grade C. [Lilly release; Zepbound label]

See tirzepatide side effects for the label figures and tirzepatide vs retatrutide for the weight-loss trials side by side.

Is retatrutide approved, and do research peptides have the same side effects?

Retatrutide is not approved by the FDA or any other regulator. Lilly plans to file for approval in early 2027. Products sold online as research peptides were not the products tested in these trials. The FDA warns they are of unknown quality and may be harmful, so trial side-effect rates do not describe them. [Lilly Medical Information; FDA]

The FDA says retatrutide cannot be used in compounding under federal law and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. [FDA]

How should I read the evidence grades?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or surrogate-only (a lab or scan measure instead of how people feel or function).
  • Grade C: animal, cell, mechanistic or anecdotal evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence.

We grade the specific claim, not the compound. One Phase 3 trial, or a surrogate outcome alone, is Grade B.
