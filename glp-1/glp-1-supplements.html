Do GLP-1 supplements work? What the evidence shows

Short answer. No supplement sold as a "GLP-1 booster" or "natural GLP-1" has been shown to work like a GLP-1 drug. Semaglutide produced an average loss of about 15% of body weight in its main trial. The best-supported supplement ingredients produce about 1 to 2 kg in pooled trials, and several produce nothing measurable.

Some ingredients do raise the body's own GLP-1 a little or modestly improve blood sugar. That is not the same thing: natural GLP-1 is broken down within minutes, while the drugs are built to last a week. We found no published trial of any GLP-1 patch, drop or gummy product.

What "GLP-1 supplement" means

GLP-1 is a gut hormone released after eating. It signals fullness and helps control blood sugar. Drugs such as semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) copy and outlast it.

A "GLP-1 supplement" contains no GLP-1 drug. The label usually means one of three things:

  • an ingredient claimed to raise your own GLP-1, such as a citrus flavonoid, a probiotic or a protein fragment;
  • a general weight or blood-sugar ingredient, such as berberine, fiber or chromium, sold under a GLP-1 name;
  • nutrition support for people taking a GLP-1 drug, such as protein or vitamins. That is a different use, covered in our guide to supplements on GLP-1 drugs.

Why a small rise in your own GLP-1 is not Ozempic

The body's own GLP-1 is "rapidly degraded," with a half-life of 2 to 3 minutes. Semaglutide is engineered to resist that breakdown: the Ozempic label gives it a half-life "of approximately 1 week." A supplement that nudges natural GLP-1 up produces a brief, small signal. A weekly injection produces a strong one that never switches off.

The trials bear this out. In a 12-week trial in 103 adults with prediabetes, the lemon flavonoid extract Eriomin raised GLP-1 by 15% and lowered blood sugar by 5%, but "did not influence the anthropometric" measures, meaning weight and waist did not change.

The benchmark

In the STEP 1 trial, 1,961 adults taking semaglutide 2.4 mg lost an average of 14.9% of body weight over 68 weeks, against 2.4% with placebo. For someone weighing 100 kg (220 lb), that is roughly 15 kg against 2.4 kg. Keep that scale in mind for the table below.

Patches, drops and gummies

Patches. A 2026 analysis in a pharmacy journal examined 24 "natural GLP-1" patch products and one gel. Their most common ingredients were berberine, glutamine, cinnamon and pomegranate. The authors pointed out that US law defines a dietary supplement as something that is swallowed, "so all these products are illegal" as supplements. None posted a certificate of analysis, many lacked the required FDA disclaimer, and many used deceptive advertising. We found no trial showing that any of these ingredients works through the skin. More detail is in Do GLP-1 patches work?

Drops and gummies. We searched PubMed for trials of GLP-1 drops, sublingual products or gummies and found none. A drop or gummy can only hold a small amount of any ingredient, so check whether the dose matches what was used in that ingredient's studies. For most ingredients in the table, the studied doses were hundreds of milligrams to several grams a day.

Salmon peptides (salmon protein hydrolysate)

Salmon protein hydrolysate is salmon protein broken into small fragments, sold under the ingredient name ProGo and marketed in some products as a natural GLP-1 and GIP activator. Here is what has been published:

  • Laboratory study (2024). In cell tests, the hydrolysate raised GLP-1 and GIP receptor activity about 2.4-fold and 2.6-fold at a concentration of 10 mg/mL. This shows activity in a dish. It does not show what happens after digestion in a person. The authors were affiliated with the ingredient's maker, which sponsored the study.
  • Pilot trial (2026). Fourteen overweight adults took 12 g a day of the hydrolysate or whey protein for 56 days. The hydrolysate group had lower BMI and body fat as secondary outcomes. The authors describe the trial as small, exploratory and "hypothesis-generating," note that diet and activity were not recorded, and include company employees.
  • Independent trial of a different salmon protein (2020). In 93 overweight adults, 2.5 g a day of salmon protein in tablets for 8 weeks "did not affect glucose and insulin concentrations."

The supportive human data used 12 g a day of powder. We found no published trial of salmon peptide drops, and no trial comparing any salmon peptide product with a GLP-1 drug. Until larger independent trials exist, the claim that it works like a GLP-1 drug is not established.

Safety and who should be careful

  • Diabetes medication. Berberine, chromium, fiber and some probiotics can lower blood sugar. Combined with insulin or sulfonylureas, that can add up. Talk to your prescriber first.
  • On a GLP-1 drug already. No "GLP-1 booster" has been tested alongside semaglutide or tirzepatide.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding. Most of these ingredients have not been tested; berberine in particular is advised against.
  • Fish allergy. Salmon peptide products are made from fish.
  • Product quality. Supplements do not have to prove they work, or prove what is in them, before sale.

Do not stop or replace a prescribed medication with a supplement without talking to your clinician.

Common questions

Do GLP-1 supplements work?

Not like the drugs. The best-supported ingredients, such as psyllium fiber and berberine, produce about 1 to 2 kg of weight loss in pooled trials, against about 15% of body weight with semaglutide. Several popular ingredients show no meaningful effect.

What is the best natural GLP-1 supplement?

No head-to-head trials exist, so there is no evidence-based "best." Psyllium fiber and berberine have the most trials behind them, for modest effects.

Do GLP-1 patches work?

We found no trial of any GLP-1 patch. A 2026 analysis of 24 patch products concluded they cannot legally be sold as dietary supplements in the US, and found no quality certificates for any of them.

Do GLP-1 drops work?

We found no published trial of GLP-1 drops. Check whether the amount in a serving comes close to the dose used in that ingredient's studies.

Can you raise GLP-1 naturally?

Yes, modestly and briefly. Eating releases GLP-1, and a lemon flavonoid extract raised it by 15 to 17% in two trials. Natural GLP-1 lasts only minutes, and that rise did not change weight.

Is there a natural Ozempic?

No. Nothing sold as a supplement has matched semaglutide in a trial, and none has been compared with it directly.

Can I take a GLP-1 supplement with Ozempic or Wegovy?

None has been tested in combination. Ingredients that lower blood sugar could add to the drug's effect, so ask your prescriber.
