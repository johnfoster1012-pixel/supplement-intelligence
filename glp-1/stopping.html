Stopping a GLP-1 drug: what happens to your weight

Evidence reviewed: November 20, 2026.

Short answer. In every large trial where people stopped semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) or tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro), most of them regained a big share of the weight within a year. Staying on the drug kept the weight off far better than stopping. That is solid evidence (grade A).

No method has been proven to stop the regain for these two drugs. This page does not tell you whether or how to stop. That is a decision to make with your prescriber. No products are linked or recommended on this page.

TL;DR

  • People who stopped semaglutide 2.4 mg in the STEP 1 trial regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost within a year (327 people). [PMID 35441470]
  • In SURMOUNT-4 (670 people), those switched from tirzepatide to placebo regained 14.0% of body weight in a year. Those who stayed on it lost another 5.5%. [PMID 38078870]
  • A 2026 review of 37 studies found weight came back at about 0.4 kg a month after weight-loss medicines stopped, faster than after diet and exercise programs. [PMID 41500720]
  • Gains in blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol also faded after stopping. [PMIDs 35441470, 41500720]
  • Exercise during treatment helped people keep weight off after stopping in one trial of an older GLP-1 drug (grade B). Tapering has no controlled evidence (grade C).

What happens to your weight after stopping semaglutide?

Two semaglutide trials answer this, and they point the same way.

STEP 1 extension. Adults took semaglutide 2.4 mg a week or placebo for 68 weeks, with lifestyle counseling. Then everything stopped, the counseling too, and 327 people were followed for another year. [PMID 35441470]

STEP 1 extensionSemaglutidePlacebo
Weight change, week 0 to 68 (on treatment)−17.3%−2.0%
Weight regained, week 68 to 120 (after stopping)11.6 percentage points1.9 percentage points
Net change, week 0 to 120−5.6%−0.1%

The authors' summary: people "regained two-thirds of their prior weight loss" within a year. They were still 5.6% lighter on average than when they started, but that is an average. Some regained more and some less.

STEP 4. This trial asked what happens if you stop while still losing. All 902 people took semaglutide for 20 weeks and lost 10.6% on average. Then 803 were randomly split: 535 stayed on semaglutide and 268 were switched to placebo. Both groups kept getting lifestyle counseling. Over the next 48 weeks, the semaglutide group lost another 7.9%. The placebo group gained back 6.9%. [PMID 33755728]

What happens when you stop tirzepatide (Zepbound or Mounjaro)?

The same thing, on a bigger scale. In SURMOUNT-4, 783 adults took tirzepatide for 36 weeks. The 670 who reached their highest tolerated dose had lost 20.9% on average. They were then randomly split, 335 to keep taking tirzepatide and 335 to switch to placebo, for another 52 weeks. Everyone had lifestyle counseling throughout. [PMID 38078870]

SURMOUNT-4Stayed on tirzepatideSwitched to placebo
Weight change, week 36 to 88−5.5%+14.0%
Overall change, week 0 to 88−25.3%−9.9%
Kept at least 80% of the weight lost89.5%16.6%

So about 1 in 6 people who stopped kept most of their loss a year later. Eli Lilly, which makes tirzepatide, funded and ran the trial.

STEP 4 and SURMOUNT-4 used different drugs, people and timelines. They were not tested against each other, so their numbers should not be compared as if they were.

How fast does the weight come back?

A University of Oxford team pooled 37 studies with 9,341 people in a review published in the BMJ in January 2026. After weight-loss medicines stopped, weight came back at about 0.4 kg (just under 1 pound) a month on average. That was faster than after diet and exercise programs, by about 0.3 kg a month, even after allowing for how much weight people had lost. [PMID 41500720] [Oxford summary]

At that pace the authors project a return to starting weight in about 1.5 to 2 years. In their summary they give a faster rate for semaglutide and tirzepatide, about 0.8 kg a month, with weight back to baseline in about 1.5 years. They also say the number of studies on those two drugs is still small, follow-up after stopping was usually short, and some figures are projections rather than measured weights.

Do blood pressure and blood sugar gains last after stopping?

Mostly not. In the STEP 1 extension, the authors wrote that the improvements seen on semaglutide "reverted towards baseline at week 120 for most variables." Blood pressure went back to starting levels. A small edge over placebo remained for HbA1c (a long-term blood sugar test) and some blood fats. [PMID 35441470]

The 2026 BMJ review projected that HbA1c, fasting blood sugar, blood pressure, cholesterol and triglycerides return to baseline within about 1.4 years of stopping. [PMID 41500720] This comes from one extension study and a modeled review, so it is some evidence, still thin (grade B). Whether stopping changes heart attack or stroke risk over the long run has not been measured.

Has anything been shown to keep weight off after stopping?

Not for semaglutide or tirzepatide in a randomized trial that we found as of November 20, 2026. Here is what has been tested, from strongest to weakest.

ApproachWhat was testedGrade
Exercise during treatmentA Copenhagen trial of liraglutide, an older daily GLP-1 drug. One year after treatment ended (109 people followed), the liraglutide-only group had regained 6.0 kg more than the exercise-only group. Over two years, liraglutide plus exercise left people 5.1 kg lighter than liraglutide alone. [PMID 38544798]B (one trial, older drug; 109 of the 166 who finished treatment came back for follow-up)
Switching to a GLP-1 pillATTAIN-MAINTAIN: 376 adults who had finished 72 weeks of injected tirzepatide or semaglutide were switched to daily orforglipron (Foundayo) or placebo. A year later, the orforglipron groups had kept 74.7% (after tirzepatide) and 79.3% (after semaglutide) of their earlier loss, against 49.2% and 37.6% on placebo. This is a switch to another GLP-1 drug, not stopping. [Nat Med 2026]B (one trial, one year)
Tapering the dose downA 2024 conference report from one digital weight-loss clinic: 353 of its 2,246 patients cut their semaglutide dose to zero over about 9 weeks with coaching. There was no comparison group, and the results were not published as a full paper. [APhA report]C (uncontrolled, one clinic)
SupplementsWe found no trial of any supplement given to people stopping semaglutide or tirzepatide. One 2026 trial tested a pasteurized Akkermansia bacteria product in 84 people after an 8-week diet, not a GLP-1 drug: they regained 13.6% of the lost weight over 24 weeks, against 32.9% on placebo. [Nat Med 2026]C for anyone coming off a GLP-1 (not tested in them)

Doctors at the Mayo Clinic, writing in American Family Physician in February 2026, note the "absence of robust evidence or guidelines" for stopping. They describe dose de-escalation, spacing out doses and adding another medicine as options to discuss, say any plan should come with renewed lifestyle changes, and say "the decision to discontinue must be individualized." [AFP editorial]

Muscle matters here too. Some of the weight lost on these drugs is lean tissue, so the regain is worth discussing with a clinician alongside strength training. See does GLP-1 use cause muscle loss?

What isn't known yet?

  • Who keeps the weight off and why. Trials report averages, and a mean isn't a promise.
  • Whether tapering, spacing out doses or a lower maintenance dose works better than stopping outright for semaglutide or tirzepatide. None has a randomized trial that we found.
  • What happens beyond about a year or two after stopping, and what it does to heart attack and stroke risk.
  • Whether people stopping compounded versions of these drugs have the same results. The trials used the approved drugs.

How should I read the evidence grades on this page?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, small, short or from a single trial.
  • Grade C: animal, lab, uncontrolled or expert-opinion evidence, or no human evidence at all.

The weight regain after stopping is grade A: three randomized trials and a 37-study review agree. The other claims are graded where they appear.

Frequently asked questions

Will I gain the weight back after stopping Ozempic or Wegovy?

Most people in trials regained a large share. In the STEP 1 extension (327 people), those who stopped semaglutide 2.4 mg regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost within a year. Results vary a lot from person to person. [PMID 35441470]

What happens when you stop tirzepatide (Zepbound or Mounjaro)?

In SURMOUNT-4, people who had lost an average of 20.9% on tirzepatide and were switched to placebo regained 14.0% of body weight over the next year, while those who stayed on tirzepatide lost another 5.5%. [PMID 38078870]

How fast does weight come back after stopping a GLP-1?

A 2026 BMJ review of 37 studies (9,341 people) found weight came back at about 0.4 kg a month on average after weight-loss medicines stopped, faster than after diet and exercise programs. At that pace the authors project a return to starting weight in about 1.5 to 2 years. [PMID 41500720]

Is there a way to keep the weight off after stopping?

No method has been proven for semaglutide or tirzepatide. One trial with an older GLP-1 drug, liraglutide, found that people who also did supervised exercise during treatment kept more weight off a year after stopping. Tapering has only small, uncontrolled reports behind it. Plans for stopping should be made with your prescriber. [PMID 38544798]

Does tapering off a GLP-1 prevent weight regain?

It has not been tested in a randomized trial. The evidence is a conference report from one digital weight-loss clinic with no comparison group, so it cannot show that tapering works better than stopping.

Do blood pressure and blood sugar get worse after stopping?

In the STEP 1 extension, most of the heart and metabolic improvements seen on semaglutide moved back toward where they started within a year of stopping. The 2026 BMJ review projected that these markers return to baseline within about 1.4 years. [PMIDs 35441470, 41500720]
