Zepbound (tirzepatide) side effects: what trials and labels show

Short answer. The most common Zepbound and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) side effects are stomach and bowel problems. In the Zepbound weight-loss trials, 56% of people on each studied dose had one, versus 30% on placebo, and nausea affected 25% to 29%. Most of it happened while the dose was being raised. The label also lists rarer serious risks. Not medical advice.

What are the most common tirzepatide side effects?

Stomach and bowel problems. In the Zepbound weight-loss trials, 56% of people on each studied dose had at least one, versus 30% on placebo. Nausea was most common at 25% to 29%, then diarrhea, constipation and vomiting. Fatigue, injection site reactions, burping and acid reflux were less common. [Zepbound label]

Are Mounjaro side effects different from Zepbound side effects?

They are the same drug, tirzepatide, so the side effects are the same kind. The Mounjaro label reports lower rates, such as nausea in 12% to 18% versus 4% on placebo, but those trials enrolled people with type 2 diabetes. Rates from separate trials should not be compared directly. [Mounjaro label; Zepbound label]

The Mounjaro label pooled two placebo-controlled type 2 diabetes trials. It lists side effects reported in at least 5% of people. Grade B.

How long do tirzepatide side effects last?

For most people in the trials, the stomach side effects came early. The Zepbound label says most nausea, vomiting and diarrhea happened while the dose was being raised and decreased over time. Most people who stopped because of side effects did so in the first few months. That is a trial average, not a promise for one person. [Zepbound label]

The label does not give a number of days or weeks that side effects last. Pages that promise a fixed timeline are going beyond what the label reports. Grade B.

How many people stop tirzepatide because of side effects?

A minority. In the Zepbound weight-loss trials, 4.8%, 6.3% and 6.7% of people on the three studied doses stopped because of side effects, versus 3.4% on placebo. Stomach and bowel side effects alone led 1.9%, 3.3% and 4.3% to stop, versus 0.5% on placebo. [Zepbound label]

In the Mounjaro type 2 diabetes trials, 3.0%, 5.4% and 6.6% stopped because of stomach and bowel side effects, versus 0.4% on placebo. [Mounjaro label] Grade B.

Does tirzepatide make you tired?

Some people reported it. In the Zepbound weight-loss trials, fatigue, which the label groups with weakness, lethargy and malaise, was reported by 5%, 6% and 7% of people on the three studied doses, versus 3% on placebo. Dizziness was reported by 4% to 5%, versus 2% on placebo. [Zepbound label]

Low blood pressure was reported in 1.6% of people on Zepbound versus 0.1% on placebo, and more often in people also taking blood pressure medicine (2.2% versus 1.2%). [Zepbound label] The label does not say what caused the tiredness. Grade B.

Does tirzepatide cause hair loss?

Hair loss was reported more often than with placebo: 5%, 4% and 5% at the three studied doses, versus 1%. The Zepbound label says it was associated with weight reduction. It was much more common in women, 7.1% on Zepbound versus 1.3% on placebo, than in men, 0.5% versus 0%. [Zepbound label]

Hair shedding after fast weight loss is a known pattern, but the label does not show that tirzepatide itself damages hair. No supplement has been shown in trials to prevent it in people on GLP-1 drugs; see our GLP-1 vitamins and supplements guide. Grade B.

Does tirzepatide cause muscle loss?

Lean mass fell along with fat. In a 160-person SURMOUNT-1 body-scan substudy, lean mass changed by −10.9% with tirzepatide versus −2.6% with placebo over 72 weeks. About 75% of the weight lost was fat and 25% was lean mass in both groups. Lean mass on a scan is not the same as muscle strength. [PMID 39996356]

In the same substudy, body weight changed by −21.3% versus −5.3% and fat mass by −33.9% versus −8.2%. These are within-group changes from one nested substudy with 124 people on tirzepatide and 36 on placebo. Grade B. For what helps keep muscle, see GLP-1 and muscle loss.

Does tirzepatide cause burping, acid reflux or gas?

Yes, in a small share of people. In the Zepbound weight-loss trials, burping was reported by 4% to 5% of people versus 1% on placebo, acid reflux by 4% to 5% versus 2%, and gas and bloating by 3% to 4% versus 2%. [Zepbound label]

The label does not describe the smell of burps, so sulfur burps are not measured there. Grade B for these rates. Reports of sulfur-smelling burps come from users and are Grade C (anecdotal).

Does tirzepatide affect birth control pills?

It can. Both labels say tirzepatide may make birth control pills less effective, because it slows how fast the stomach empties. The labels advise switching to a non-pill method or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose increase. Ask the prescriber before changing anything. [Zepbound label; Mounjaro label]

In a drug-interaction study described on the Mounjaro label, peak levels of the hormones in a combined birth control pill fell by 55% to 66% after a tirzepatide dose, and total exposure fell by 20% to 23%. [Mounjaro label] Grade B.

Does tirzepatide raise heart rate?

Slightly, on average. The Zepbound label reports a mean rise of 1 to 3 beats per minute, versus no rise with placebo. The Mounjaro label reports 2 to 4 beats per minute, versus 1 with placebo. [Zepbound label; Mounjaro label]

In the Mounjaro trials, a rise of 15 or more beats per minute was seen in 4.6%, 5.9% and 10% of people at the three studied doses, versus 4.3% on placebo. [Mounjaro label] Grade B. These are averages from the trials, not a measure of risk for one person.

What serious risks are on the FDA label?

The Zepbound label has a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rats. It also warns about severe stomach reactions, kidney injury from dehydration, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, serious allergic reactions, low blood sugar, diabetic eye complications, decrease or loss of vision in one eye (NAION) and inhaling stomach contents during anesthesia. This is not a complete list. [Zepbound label]

Thyroid tumor warning

In rats, tirzepatide causes thyroid C-cell tumors at clinically relevant exposures. It is unknown whether it causes these tumors in humans. The label says not to use it with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2, or with a known serious allergy to it. Grade C for human tumor risk (animal data).

Vision loss (NAION)

A November 2026 Zepbound label update added a warning about decrease or loss of vision in one eye from a rare optic nerve problem called NAION. The FDA said in November 2026 that it is requiring this warning on all GLP-1 drug labels. As read on November 26, 2026, the Mounjaro label's warnings list did not include it yet.

Mood and suicidal thoughts

The Zepbound label's warning about suicidal behavior and ideation was removed in February 2026, according to the label's list of recent major changes. Anyone with new or worsening mood changes should still tell their prescriber.

Reports after approval

Postmarketing reports include pancreatitis, ileus, intestinal blockage, severe constipation, serious allergy, pulmonary aspiration and kidney failure. Their frequency cannot be reliably estimated. Grade C (spontaneous reports).

Is tirzepatide FDA-approved, and for what?

Yes. Mounjaro is approved for blood sugar control in type 2 diabetes from age 10 and to lower heart-event risk in high-risk adults with type 2 diabetes. Zepbound is approved for long-term weight reduction in adults with obesity, or overweight plus a related condition, and for moderate-to-severe sleep apnea with obesity. [Mounjaro label; Zepbound label]

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly injection that acts on two gut hormone receptors, GIP and GLP-1. The labels do not recommend using it together with other tirzepatide products or any other GLP-1 drug.

How should I read the evidence grades?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome.
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or surrogate-only (a lab or scan measure instead of how people feel or function).
  • Grade C: animal, cell, mechanistic or anecdotal evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence.

We grade the specific claim, not the compound. One Phase 3 trial, or a surrogate outcome alone, is Grade B.
