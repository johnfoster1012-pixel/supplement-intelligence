Retatrutide doses tested in clinical trials (not a dosing guide)

Evidence reviewed: November 15, 2026.

Retatrutide is investigational, is not FDA-approved, and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. It cannot be compounded under federal law. There is no approved retatrutide dose. [FDA]

This page reports what clinical trials assigned. It is not a dosing guide and not medical advice.

TL;DR

  • There is no approved retatrutide dosing schedule. Everything below describes research protocols run under medical supervision. [FDA]
  • In TRIUMPH-1, mean weight change at 80 weeks was −17.6%, −23.7% and −25.0% with 4, 9 and 12 mg versus −3.9% with placebo (weight loss versus placebo is Grade A, because several randomized trials agree). [PMID 42814954]
  • Retatrutide's half-life was about 6 days in a small Phase 1b study (Grade B). [PMID 36354040]

How were trial treatments managed?

Trial staff adjusted assigned treatment under medical supervision. There is no approved retatrutide dosing schedule. [PMID 41090431] [NCT04881760]

What did the Phase 1b retatrutide study test?

The Phase 1b study enrolled 72 adults with type 2 diabetes. Stomach and bowel problems were the most common adverse events. Grade B. [PMID 36354040]

What is the half-life of retatrutide?

A small Phase 1b study estimated retatrutide’s half-life at about six days. [PMID 36354040]

What side effects were reported?

The Phase 2 obesity trial reported dose-related gastrointestinal side effects, mostly mild or moderate. [PMID 37366315] See reported retatrutide side effects for more detail.

How much retatrutide should I take?

No personal retatrutide dose has been established, and none of the sources on this page supply one. Retatrutide is not approved, the FDA says it has not been found safe and effective for any condition, and it cannot be compounded under federal law. [FDA] The FDA has also warned companies that illegally sold unapproved drugs containing retatrutide that were falsely labeled “for research purposes” or “not for human consumption.” Those products do not carry the trials' evidence or quality controls. [FDA]

Lilly says retatrutide has not been approved by the FDA or any other regulator and plans to submit an application in the first quarter of 2027, with no guarantee of approval. That is a company statement (Grade B). [Lilly Medical Information] Anyone weighing a weight-loss medicine can ask a licensed clinician about approved options.

How should I read the evidence grades on this page?

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome (more than one randomized trial agreeing, or a pooled analysis of consistent trials).
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or surrogate-only (for example, a lab or imaging measure instead of how people feel or function).
  • Grade C: animal, lab, cell, mechanistic, uncontrolled, anecdotal or expert-opinion evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence.
