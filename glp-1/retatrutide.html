Retatrutide: what it does, side effects, and vs tirzepatide

Evidence reviewed: November 10, 2026.

Retatrutide is investigational, is not FDA-approved, and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. It cannot be compounded under federal law. This page is not medical advice. FDA

The published trials report weight loss and improvements in some other measured outcomes. That evidence does not establish safe use outside trials or make products sold online reliable.

TL;DR

  • Retatrutide is investigational, is not FDA-approved, and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. It cannot be compounded under federal law.
  • In TRIUMPH-1, adults without diabetes assigned to 4, 9, or 12 mg had mean weight changes of −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0%, versus −3.9% with placebo at 80 weeks, using the treatment-regimen analysis. These are Grade A results within that trial, not predictions for an individual.
  • Gastrointestinal problems were the most common adverse events. TRIUMPH-2 also reported hypotension and dysesthesia, or altered skin sensation. Phase 2 reported heart-rate increases.
  • No published head-to-head trial of retatrutide versus tirzepatide was identified as of November 10, 2026. Figures from separate trials do not establish that one drug is superior.
  • FDA has warned about illegal online sales of unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research use.

Frequently asked questions

Is retatrutide FDA-approved?

No. As of November 10, 2026, retatrutide is not an FDA-approved drug, and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration states that retatrutide and cagrilintide “cannot be used in compounding under federal law” and “have not been found safe and effective for any condition” (https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss).

How do retatrutide and tirzepatide compare in trials?

In TRIUMPH-1, treatment-regimen (intention-to-treat) mean weight change at 80 weeks was −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0% with retatrutide 4, 9, and 12 mg, versus −3.9% with placebo. In SURMOUNT-1, the same estimand at 72 weeks was −15.0%, −19.5%, and −20.9% with tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg, versus −3.1% with placebo. No head-to-head trial has been published. [PMIDs 42814954, 35658024]

What retatrutide doses did the clinical trials use?

The Phase 2 obesity trial studied weekly maintenance doses of 1, 4, 8, and 12 mg. The Phase 2 diabetes trial studied 0.5, 4, 8, and 12 mg. TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 studied 4, 9, and 12 mg; TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4 studied 9 and 12 mg. These are trial assignments, not instructions for use. [PMIDs 37366315, 37385280, 41090431]

How much weight loss did retatrutide show in trials?

In TRIUMPH-1, adults without diabetes assigned to 4, 9, or 12 mg had mean weight changes of −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0%, versus −3.9% with placebo at 80 weeks, using the treatment-regimen analysis. These are Grade A results within that trial, not predictions for an individual. [PMID 42814954]

What side effects were reported with retatrutide?

Gastrointestinal problems were the most common adverse events. TRIUMPH-2 also reported hypotension and dysesthesia, or altered skin sensation. Phase 2 reported heart-rate increases. The dedicated knee osteoarthritis paper reported more serious adverse events with retatrutide than placebo in both trials it analyzed. [PMIDs 37366315, 42810372, 43001755]

Can retatrutide be compounded or bought online?

Retatrutide cannot be compounded under federal law. FDA has warned about illegal sales of unapproved products containing retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research purposes or not for human consumption. Those labels do not establish safety or quality. [FDA; reference 9]

How should I read the retatrutide evidence?

Our evidence posture is cautious: distinguish published results from study plans, and keep each finding tied to the people and outcomes actually studied.

  • Grade A: a published Phase 3 randomized trial, for outcomes reported within that trial.
  • Grade B: a published Phase 2 randomized trial; clearly labeled exploratory, nested, or subgroup analyses; design-only papers; registry results absent from the journal abstract; or clearly labeled company statements.
  • Grade C: registered trials without published outcomes, predictions based on how a drug works, and comparisons between separate trials that did not match participants and methods.

The published Phase 3 pain and sleep apnea outcomes described below are Grade A within their trials. That grade does not extend to other populations or comparisons with another drug.

What is retatrutide?

Retatrutide, also called LY3437943, is a single peptide studied as a weekly injection under the skin. It activates three receptors: GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon. Tirzepatide activates GIP and GLP-1 receptors. This is established pharmacology described in published trial reports, not a claim that the extra receptor makes retatrutide better. [PMIDs 37366315, 35658024]

What does the evidence say retatrutide does?

  • A: TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 reported greater average weight loss with retatrutide than placebo in adults without and with type 2 diabetes, respectively. [PMIDs 42814954, 42810372]
  • A: published Phase 3 studies reported improvements in knee osteoarthritis pain and sleep apnea breathing events in the groups studied. [PMIDs 43001755, 42904878]
  • B: earlier Phase 2 studies reported weight loss and, in type 2 diabetes, lower HbA1c, a measure of blood sugar over time. [PMIDs 37366315, 37385280]
  • C: a claim that retatrutide is better than tirzepatide is not established by these separate trials. TRIUMPH-5 has no published results. [NCT06662383]

What retatrutide doses did the trials use?

These are descriptions of research protocols, not instructions for taking retatrutide.

Phase 2 obesity

The trial enrolled 338 adults with obesity, or overweight plus a weight-related condition, for 48 weeks. The following weekly injection arms were described in the registry:

Some groups increased doses up to week 12. The verified source text does not establish the time between increases. Results labeled “combined 4 mg” and “combined 8 mg” pool the two starting-dose groups for each maintenance dose. Grade B. [PMID 37366315; NCT04881760]

Phase 2 type 2 diabetes

The trial used weekly retatrutide maintenance doses of 0.5, 4, 8, or 12 mg, placebo, or dulaglutide 1.5 mg. The 4 mg groups started at 2 mg or had no escalation; the 8 mg groups started at 2 mg (“slow escalation”) or 4 mg (“fast escalation”); the 12 mg group started at 2 mg. These study labels do not supply a complete timing schedule. Grade B. [PMID 37385280]

In the obesity trial, the main outcome was the least-squares mean percent change in body weight at 24 weeks. Reported values were −7.2% with 1 mg, −12.9% with combined 4 mg, −17.3% with combined 8 mg, and −17.5% with 12 mg, versus −1.6% with placebo. At 48 weeks:

At 48 weeks, a weight reduction of 5% or more, 10% or more, and 15% or more was reported in 92%, 75%, and 60% of participants who received 4 mg; 100%, 91%, and 75% of those who received 8 mg; 100%, 93%, and 83% of those who received 12 mg; and 27%, 9%, and 2% of those who received placebo. TRIUMPH-1 randomized 2339 participants. Differences versus placebo for the 9 mg and 12 mg groups, the primary dose comparisons, were −19.8 and −21.0 percentage points (P<0.001 for both). TRIUMPH-2 randomized 1152 participants; 965 (84%) completed the study drug. Estimated treatment differences versus placebo were −6.9% (95% CI −8.7 to −5.1) with 4 mg, −11.8% (−13.7 to −9.8) with 9 mg, and −13.8% (−15.8 to −11.8) with 12 mg (p<0.0001 for all). Grade A within each trial. The columns describe different studies and populations. The TRIUMPH-2 abstract describes improved blood sugar control but does not give numeric HbA1c changes. [PMIDs 42814954, 42810372]

Knee osteoarthritis pain

TRIUMPH-4 enrolled 445 participants and measured pain at 68 weeks. In the dedicated paper, the WOMAC pain scale runs from 0 to 10, with higher scores meaning worse pain. Under the paper’s hybrid-treatment analysis, changes were −3.96 with 9 mg and −3.74 with 12 mg, versus −2.12 with placebo. Differences from placebo were −1.83 and −1.61 points. The intention-to-treat (ITT) differences were −1.70 and −1.38 points.

Weight changes using the treatment-policy analysis were −20.0%, −23.7%, and −4.6%, respectively. The same paper also reports TRIUMPH-1’s knee osteoarthritis group (574 participants) at 80 weeks. It describes the WOMAC pain scale as 0 to 10; the main TRIUMPH-1 paper describes the scale as 1 to 10. Those source descriptions should not be silently treated as identical. In the dedicated paper, hybrid-treatment changes in that TRIUMPH-1 group were −3.22, −3.48, and −3.64 with 4, 9, and 12 mg, versus −1.87 with placebo. Grade A for the reported within-trial outcomes. [PMID 43001755]

Obstructive sleep apnea

The dedicated paper reports 243 participants without diabetes (TRIUMPH-1-OSA) and 448 with diabetes (TRIUMPH-2-OSA). It measured the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI): breathing interruptions per hour. Baseline mean AHI was 58.6 and 55.9 events per hour, respectively.

For the 9 and 12 mg groups without diabetes, hybrid-treatment differences from placebo at 80 weeks were −24.4 and −21.9 events per hour. ITT differences were −24.7 and −22.5. With diabetes, the corresponding hybrid differences were −14.4 and −15.1; ITT differences were −13.9 and −15.0. Both analyses are reported because they answer the outcome question using different rules. As a key secondary outcome, mean percent change in body weight (treatment-regimen analysis) was −12.5% to −22.2% in TRIUMPH-1-OSA and −12.1% to −18.5% in TRIUMPH-2-OSA, versus −4.8% and −5.6% with placebo; the abstract reports those as ranges across retatrutide doses, not as a dose-by-dose split. Grade A within these trials. [PMID 42904878]

Trials with no published outcomes identified

TRIUMPH-3 was completed on ClinicalTrials.gov, but no peer-reviewed outcomes paper was identified as of November 10, 2026. TRIUMPH-5, comparing retatrutide with tirzepatide, was active but not recruiting, with no posted results. Estimated completion dates are not publications. Grade C for claims depending on these unpublished outcomes. [NCT05882045; NCT06662383]

What side effects were reported with retatrutide?

In Phase 2 obesity, stomach and bowel problems were the most common adverse events. They were dose-related, mostly mild to moderate, and partly reduced by a lower starting dose. Heart-rate increases peaked at 24 weeks and then declined; the abstract does not provide beats-per-minute figures. Grade B. [PMID 37366315]

The registry’s posted Phase 2 safety results extend through safety follow-up, up to 52 weeks, among participants who received a dose. These small-arm registry counts are Grade B, and are not from the journal abstract. Nausea was reported in:

Vomiting in the two 8 mg starting-dose groups was 2/35 and 9/35 (25.7%), respectively, and 12/62 (19.4%) in the 12 mg group. [NCT04881760]

In the Phase 2 diabetes trial, mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation) were reported in 67 (35%) of 190 participants in the retatrutide groups, ranging from 6 (13%) of 47 in the 0.5 mg group to 12 (50%) of 24 in the 8 mg fast-escalation group, compared with 6 (13%) of 45 with placebo and 16 (35%) of 46 with dulaglutide 1.5 mg. There were no reports of severe hypoglycemia and no deaths during that study. Grade B. [PMID 37385280]

In TRIUMPH-2, diarrhea occurred in 80/292 (27%), 95/284 (34%), and 96/286 (34%) in the 4, 9, and 12 mg groups, versus 38/287 (13%) with placebo. Nausea occurred in 40 (14%), 59 (21%), and 80 (28%) versus 23 (8%). Hypotension occurred in 4 (1%), 14 (5%), and 18 (6%) versus 1 (<1%). Dysesthesia, or altered skin sensation, occurred in 13 (4%), 16 (6%), and 21 (7%) versus 2 (1%). The safety denominators differ from the randomized totals; the 12 mg gastrointestinal rates used 286 participants rather than the 287 randomized. Permanent treatment discontinuation because of adverse events or death was reported in 11 (4%), 33 (12%), and 22 (8%) of the retatrutide groups, versus 14 (5%) with placebo. Deaths occurred in 2, 3, and 1 participants in the retatrutide groups and 1 with placebo; investigators judged all of those deaths unrelated to the study intervention. Grade A for these within-trial findings. [PMID 42810372]

The dedicated knee osteoarthritis paper reported more serious adverse events with retatrutide than placebo in both trials it analyzed. Its abstract did not supply percentages. Trial findings do not establish long-term safety outside research. [PMID 43001755]

How do retatrutide and tirzepatide compare in trials?

No published head-to-head trial was identified as of November 10, 2026. TRIUMPH-5 is registered and has not published results. The figures below come from separate trials and do not establish that one drug is superior. Unmatched superiority claims are Grade C.

In TRIUMPH-1, treatment-regimen (intention-to-treat) mean weight change at 80 weeks was −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0% with retatrutide 4, 9, and 12 mg, versus −3.9% with placebo. These are Grade A within TRIUMPH-1. [PMID 42814954]

In SURMOUNT-1, the same estimand at 72 weeks was −15.0%, −19.5%, and −20.9% with tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg, versus −3.1% with placebo. These are Grade A within SURMOUNT-1. [PMID 35658024]

The trials also differed in duration (80 versus 72 weeks), dose families, protocols, and background counselling.

What is still unknown about retatrutide?

Long-term safety outside trials, cardiovascular outcomes, and routine-care use are not established. The TRIUMPH-3 design included a planned DXA body-composition substudy of about 100 participants; this review has no results to report. It does not establish a lean-mass effect. [PMID 41090431]

Other gaps include unpublished TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-5 outcomes, numeric HbA1c changes missing from the fetched TRIUMPH-2 abstract, and the exact Phase 3 escalation steps absent from the fetched design-paper body text.

A Lilly Medical Information page, last reviewed August 4, 2026, states that Lilly plans to submit a Biologics License Application in the first quarter of 2027. That is a company plan, not an approval. The same page says retatrutide has not been approved by the FDA or any other regulatory authority, and that there is no guarantee of approval or of meeting any anticipated submission timeline. Grade B as a labeled company statement. [reference 11]

FDA has warned about illegal online sales of unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research use. Their quality is unknown. Retatrutide cannot be compounded under federal law. [FDA; reference 9]
