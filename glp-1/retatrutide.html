Retatrutide: what it does and what the trials show

Evidence reviewed: November 15, 2026.

Retatrutide is investigational, is not FDA-approved, and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. It cannot be compounded under federal law. This page is not medical advice. FDA

The published trials report weight loss and improvements in some other measured outcomes. That evidence does not establish safe use outside trials or make products sold online reliable.

TL;DR

  • Retatrutide is investigational, is not FDA-approved, and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. It cannot be compounded under federal law.
  • In TRIUMPH-1, adults without diabetes assigned to 4, 9, or 12 mg had mean weight changes of −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0%, versus −3.9% with placebo at 80 weeks, using the treatment-regimen analysis. Weight loss versus placebo is Grade A because several randomized trials agree; these averages are not predictions for an individual.
  • Retatrutide side effects: stomach and bowel problems were the most common. In TRIUMPH-2, nausea affected 14% to 28% of the retatrutide groups versus 8% with placebo. TRIUMPH-2 also reported low blood pressure (hypotension) and dysesthesia, an altered skin sensation. Phase 2 reported heart-rate increases. Full side-effect data.
  • No published head-to-head trial of retatrutide versus tirzepatide was identified as of November 15, 2026. Figures from separate trials do not establish that one drug is superior. See tirzepatide vs retatrutide.
  • Trial dosing protocols are summarized on the retatrutide dosing schedule page. They describe research, not instructions.
  • FDA has warned about illegal online sales of unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research use.

Frequently asked questions

Is retatrutide FDA-approved?

No. As of November 10, 2026, retatrutide is not an FDA-approved drug, and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration states that retatrutide and cagrilintide “cannot be used in compounding under federal law” and “have not been found safe and effective for any condition” (https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss).

How do retatrutide and tirzepatide compare in trials?

No head-to-head trial has been published. In separate trials, TRIUMPH-1 reported mean weight changes of −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0% with retatrutide 4, 9, and 12 mg at 80 weeks (placebo −3.9%), and SURMOUNT-1 reported −15.0%, −19.5%, and −20.9% with tirzepatide 5, 10, and 15 mg at 72 weeks (placebo −3.1%). Separate trials cannot show which drug is better. Details: tirzepatide vs retatrutide. [PMIDs 42814954, 35658024]

What retatrutide doses did the clinical trials use?

The Phase 2 obesity trial studied weekly maintenance doses of 1, 4, 8, and 12 mg. The Phase 2 diabetes trial studied 0.5, 4, 8, and 12 mg. TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 studied 4, 9, and 12 mg; TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4 studied 9 and 12 mg. These are trial assignments, not instructions for use. Details: retatrutide dosing schedule in trials. [PMIDs 37366315, 37385280, 41090431]

How much weight loss did retatrutide show in trials?

In TRIUMPH-1, adults without diabetes assigned to 4, 9, or 12 mg had mean weight changes of −17.6%, −23.7%, and −25.0%, versus −3.9% with placebo at 80 weeks, using the treatment-regimen analysis. Weight loss versus placebo is Grade A because several randomized trials agree; these averages are not predictions for an individual. [PMID 42814954]

What side effects were reported with retatrutide?

Gastrointestinal problems were the most common adverse events. In TRIUMPH-2, diarrhea affected 27% to 34% and nausea 14% to 28% of the retatrutide groups, versus 13% and 8% with placebo. TRIUMPH-2 also reported hypotension and dysesthesia, or altered skin sensation. Phase 2 reported heart-rate increases. The dedicated knee osteoarthritis paper reported more serious adverse events with retatrutide than placebo in both trials it analyzed. [PMIDs 37366315, 42810372, 43001755]

What is dysesthesia on retatrutide?

Dysesthesia is an unusual or unpleasant skin sensation, such as tingling or burning. It was reported in 4% to 7% of the retatrutide groups in TRIUMPH-2 versus 1% with placebo. Lilly’s TRIUMPH-1 release reported 5.1% to 12.5% versus 0.9%, and said most events were mild to moderate and most resolved during treatment. The cause is not established. [PMID 42810372; Lilly release]

How many people stopped retatrutide because of side effects?

In TRIUMPH-2, permanent discontinuation because of adverse events or death was 4%, 12%, and 8% with 4, 9, and 12 mg, versus 5% with placebo (Grade B, one trial). Lilly reported 4.1%, 6.9%, and 11.3% versus 4.9% in TRIUMPH-1 (Grade B, company release). [PMID 42810372; Lilly release]

Can retatrutide be compounded or bought online?

Retatrutide cannot be compounded under federal law. FDA has warned about illegal sales of unapproved products containing retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research purposes or not for human consumption. Those labels do not establish safety or quality. [FDA; reference 9]

How should I read the retatrutide evidence?

Our evidence posture is cautious: distinguish published results from study plans, and keep each finding tied to the people and outcomes actually studied.

  • Grade A: consistent randomized human evidence for that exact outcome (more than one randomized trial agreeing, or a pooled analysis of consistent trials).
  • Grade B: human evidence that is limited, mixed, short, small, from a single trial, or surrogate-only (for example, a lab or imaging measure instead of how people feel or function).
  • Grade C: animal, lab, cell, mechanistic, uncontrolled, anecdotal or expert-opinion evidence, comparisons between separate trials, or no human evidence.

Under this key, an outcome that rests on one randomized trial, even a large Phase 3 trial, is Grade B, and so is an outcome measured only by a surrogate. Knee osteoarthritis pain described below is Grade A because two randomized trials agree. Sleep apnea breathing events are Grade B because the apnea–hypopnea index is a measured breathing count, not how people feel or function. No grade extends to other populations or to comparisons with another drug.

What is retatrutide?

Retatrutide, also called LY3437943, is a single peptide studied as a weekly injection under the skin. It activates three receptors: GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon. Tirzepatide activates GIP and GLP-1 receptors. This is established pharmacology described in published trial reports, not a claim that the extra receptor makes retatrutide better. [PMIDs 37366315, 35658024]

What does the evidence say retatrutide does?

  • A: TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 reported greater average weight loss with retatrutide than placebo in adults without and with type 2 diabetes, respectively. [PMIDs 42814954, 42810372]
  • A: two randomized Phase 3 trials, reported in one paper, found improvements in knee osteoarthritis pain in the groups studied. [PMID 43001755]
  • B: published Phase 3 studies reported fewer sleep apnea breathing events, a measured index (surrogate) rather than how people feel. [PMID 42904878]
  • B: earlier Phase 2 studies reported weight loss and, in type 2 diabetes, lower HbA1c, a measure of blood sugar over time. [PMIDs 37366315, 37385280]
  • C: a claim that retatrutide is better than tirzepatide is not established by these separate trials. TRIUMPH-5 has no published results. [NCT06662383]

What retatrutide doses did the trials use?

These are descriptions of research protocols, not instructions for taking retatrutide. Phase 2 trials assigned weekly maintenance doses of 1, 4, 8, or 12 mg (obesity) and 0.5, 4, 8, or 12 mg (type 2 diabetes). TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2 assigned 4, 9, or 12 mg; TRIUMPH-3 and TRIUMPH-4 assigned 9 or 12 mg. Lilly reports that TRIUMPH-1 participants started at 2 mg once weekly and stepped up every four weeks to their assigned dose. Grade B for design papers and company protocol descriptions. [PMIDs 37366315, 37385280, 41090431; Lilly release]

The full trial-by-trial breakdown, the TRIUMPH-1 escalation steps, and what the trials did when people had side effects are on the retatrutide dosing schedule page.

TRIUMPH-1 randomized 2339 participants. Differences versus placebo for the 9 mg and 12 mg groups, the primary dose comparisons, were −19.8 and −21.0 percentage points (P<0.001 for both). TRIUMPH-2 randomized 1152 participants; 965 (84%) completed the study drug. Estimated treatment differences versus placebo were −6.9% (95% CI −8.7 to −5.1) with 4 mg, −11.8% (−13.7 to −9.8) with 9 mg, and −13.8% (−15.8 to −11.8) with 12 mg (p<0.0001 for all). The TRIUMPH-2 abstract describes improved blood sugar control but does not give numeric HbA1c changes. [PMIDs 42814954, 42810372]

Knee osteoarthritis pain

TRIUMPH-4 enrolled 445 participants and measured pain at 68 weeks. In the dedicated paper, the WOMAC pain scale runs from 0 to 10, with higher scores meaning worse pain. Under the paper’s hybrid-treatment analysis, changes were −3.96 with 9 mg and −3.74 with 12 mg, versus −2.12 with placebo. Differences from placebo were −1.83 and −1.61 points. The intention-to-treat (ITT) differences were −1.70 and −1.38 points.

Weight changes using the treatment-policy analysis were −20.0%, −23.7%, and −4.6%, respectively. The same paper also reports TRIUMPH-1’s knee osteoarthritis group (574 participants) at 80 weeks. It describes the WOMAC pain scale as 0 to 10; the main TRIUMPH-1 paper describes the scale as 1 to 10. Those source descriptions should not be silently treated as identical. In the dedicated paper, hybrid-treatment changes in that TRIUMPH-1 group were −3.22, −3.48, and −3.64 with 4, 9, and 12 mg, versus −1.87 with placebo. Grade A for knee pain, because the two randomized trials (TRIUMPH-4 and the TRIUMPH-1 knee group) agree. [PMID 43001755]

Obstructive sleep apnea

The dedicated paper reports 243 participants without diabetes (TRIUMPH-1-OSA) and 448 with diabetes (TRIUMPH-2-OSA). It measured the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI): breathing interruptions per hour. Baseline mean AHI was 58.6 and 55.9 events per hour, respectively.

For the 9 and 12 mg groups without diabetes, hybrid-treatment differences from placebo at 80 weeks were −24.4 and −21.9 events per hour. ITT differences were −24.7 and −22.5. With diabetes, the corresponding hybrid differences were −14.4 and −15.1; ITT differences were −13.9 and −15.0. Both analyses are reported because they answer the outcome question using different rules. As a key secondary outcome, mean percent change in body weight (treatment-regimen analysis) was −12.5% to −22.2% in TRIUMPH-1-OSA and −12.1% to −18.5% in TRIUMPH-2-OSA, versus −4.8% and −5.6% with placebo; the abstract reports those as ranges across retatrutide doses, not as a dose-by-dose split. Grade B for breathing events: the AHI is a measured index (a surrogate), and the abstract reports no symptom outcomes. Weight loss versus placebo in these groups agrees with the other trials (Grade A). [PMID 42904878]

Trials with no peer-reviewed outcomes yet

TRIUMPH-3 is listed as completed on ClinicalTrials.gov. Lilly released top-line TRIUMPH-3 results in July 2026 as company data, not yet in a peer-reviewed journal; as of November 10, 2026, we found no peer-reviewed outcomes paper. This page does not report figures from that company release. [Lilly July 2026 release, company source] TRIUMPH-5, comparing retatrutide with tirzepatide, was active but not recruiting, with no posted results. Estimated completion dates are not publications. Grade C for claims depending on outcomes that have not been released. [NCT05882045; NCT06662383]

What are the side effects of retatrutide?

Across the published trials, the most common retatrutide side effects were stomach and bowel problems: nausea, diarrhea, constipation, and vomiting. They were more common at higher doses, and in Phase 2 they were mostly mild to moderate. Two less familiar effects also appeared more often than with placebo: dysesthesia, an altered skin sensation, and low blood pressure (hypotension). These are trial findings, not a full safety profile. Long-term safety outside trials is not established. The full breakdown, by trial and dose, is on retatrutide side effects: what the TRIUMPH trials show.

What did Phase 2 show about side effects?

In Phase 2 obesity, stomach and bowel problems were the most common adverse events. They were dose-related, mostly mild to moderate, and partly reduced by a lower starting dose. Heart-rate increases peaked at 24 weeks and then declined; the abstract does not provide beats-per-minute figures. Grade B. [PMID 37366315]

Registry safety results for that trial (up to 52 weeks) show how much the starting dose mattered: nausea was reported in 6/35 (17.1%) of the 8 mg group that started at 2 mg, versus 21/35 (60.0%) of the 8 mg group that started at 4 mg, and 8/70 (11.4%) with placebo. These small-arm registry counts are Grade B. [NCT04881760]

In the Phase 2 diabetes trial, mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal adverse events were reported in 67 (35%) of 190 participants in the retatrutide groups, ranging from 6 (13%) of 47 at 0.5 mg to 12 (50%) of 24 in the 8 mg fast-escalation group, compared with 6 (13%) of 45 with placebo and 16 (35%) of 46 with dulaglutide 1.5 mg. There were no reports of severe hypoglycemia and no deaths. Grade B. [PMID 37385280]

Were serious side effects reported?

The dedicated knee osteoarthritis paper reported more serious adverse events with retatrutide than placebo in both trials it analyzed. Its abstract did not supply percentages. Trial findings do not establish long-term safety outside research. [PMID 43001755]

Products sold online as “research peptides” were not the products tested in these trials. Their contents and purity are unknown, so trial side-effect rates do not describe them. [FDA; reference 9]

How do retatrutide and tirzepatide compare in trials?

No published head-to-head trial was identified as of November 15, 2026. TRIUMPH-5, the trial designed to compare them, has not published results. In separate trials, TRIUMPH-1 reported −25.0% mean weight change with retatrutide 12 mg at 80 weeks, and SURMOUNT-1 reported −20.9% with tirzepatide 15 mg at 72 weeks. Those trials differed in length, doses, and participants, so a superiority claim is Grade C. [PMIDs 42814954, 35658024]

The full comparison, including the type 2 diabetes trials and side effects, is on tirzepatide vs retatrutide: what separate trials show.

What is still unknown about retatrutide?

Long-term safety outside trials, cardiovascular outcomes, and routine-care use are not established. The TRIUMPH-3 design included a planned DXA body-composition substudy of about 100 participants; this review has no results to report. It does not establish a lean-mass effect. [PMID 41090431]

Other gaps include TRIUMPH-3 results that are not yet in a journal, unpublished TRIUMPH-5 outcomes, and HbA1c numbers that the TRIUMPH-2 abstract does not give. The TRIUMPH-1 escalation steps come from a Lilly release, not yet from a peer-reviewed paper.

A Lilly Medical Information page, last reviewed August 4, 2026, states that Lilly plans to submit a Biologics License Application in the first quarter of 2027. That is a company plan, not an approval. The same page says retatrutide has not been approved by the FDA or any other regulatory authority, and that there is no guarantee of approval or of meeting any anticipated submission timeline. Grade B as a labeled company statement. [reference 11]

FDA has warned about illegal online sales of unapproved retatrutide, including products falsely labeled for research use. Their quality is unknown. Retatrutide cannot be compounded under federal law. [FDA; reference 9]
