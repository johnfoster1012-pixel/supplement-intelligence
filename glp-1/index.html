Supplements on GLP-1 drugs: what has evidence

Short answer. We searched PubMed for randomized trials of creatine, protein supplements, fiber, magnesium, biotin, collagen and berberine in people taking semaglutide or tirzepatide, and found none. Everything below is borrowed from other groups of people, such as older adults doing resistance training or adults with chronic constipation.

That borrowed evidence is reasonable for a few uses and absent for most products sold as "GLP-1 support". Nothing here replaces advice from the clinician who prescribes your medication.

Why supplements come up at all

GLP-1 drugs cause large weight loss. In the STEP 1 trial, adults taking semaglutide 2.4 mg lost an average of 14.9% of body weight over 68 weeks, against 2.4% with placebo. In SURMOUNT-1, tirzepatide produced average losses of 15.0% to 20.9% over 72 weeks, depending on dose, against 3.1% with placebo.

Two things follow from that. Some of the weight lost is lean tissue, and the drugs commonly cause stomach and bowel side effects. In the trials summarized on the Wegovy label, adults taking semaglutide 2.4 mg reported:

Most supplement questions trace back to one of these: muscle, constipation or hair.

Muscle: resistance training and protein first, creatine second

How much lean tissue is lost

In a body-composition substudy of SURMOUNT-1 (160 participants scanned by DXA), about 75% of the weight lost was fat and about 25% was lean mass. The split was the same in the tirzepatide and placebo groups, which suggests it reflects weight loss in general, not the drug specifically.

A 2026 systematic review in Annals of Internal Medicine covered 35 randomized trials of incretin drugs. The median share of weight loss coming from muscle-related measures was 28.3%, with a wide range between studies. The reviewers noted that no trial reported objective measures of physical function, so it is not yet known whether this loss affects strength or mobility.

What expert groups recommend

A 2025 joint advisory from four US lifestyle medicine, nutrition and obesity societies names "preserving muscle and bone mass through resistance training and appropriate diet" as a priority during GLP-1 treatment, along with preventing nutrient deficiencies when appetite and food intake fall. Protein targets depend on body size, age and kidney function, so they are best set with a clinician or dietitian. Protein powders are a convenience for people who cannot eat enough; we found no trial testing them in people on these drugs.

Creatine

Creatine has not been tested in a randomized trial in people taking GLP-1 drugs. The case for it is indirect: a meta-analysis of 22 randomized trials in 721 older adults found that creatine taken during resistance training added about 1.4 kg more lean tissue than training with placebo, with modest strength gains. Two limits matter. The benefit was shown with resistance training, not instead of it. And the participants were not losing weight on medication. See our creatine evidence page for safety context.

Constipation: fiber and magnesium have evidence, in other populations

Neither has been studied for constipation caused by GLP-1 drugs. Both have trial evidence in adults with chronic constipation:

  • Fiber. A meta-analysis of 16 randomized trials (1,251 adults) found 66% responded to fiber supplements against 41% on control. Psyllium was among the fibers with a clear effect, and benefit appeared only at doses above 10 g a day.
  • Magnesium oxide. In a small placebo-controlled trial (34 women, 1.5 g a day for 28 days), 70.6% improved on magnesium oxide against 25.0% on placebo.

The 2023 joint guideline from the American Gastroenterological Association and American College of Gastroenterology gives conditional recommendations for fiber and magnesium oxide in chronic constipation, and a strong recommendation for polyethylene glycol, an over-the-counter laxative that is not a supplement.

Cautions. Fiber can add to bloating, which GLP-1 drugs already cause in some people. Magnesium is cleared by the kidneys, so people with reduced kidney function should not use magnesium laxatives without medical advice. Severe or persistent constipation, or constipation with vomiting and abdominal pain, needs prompt medical attention, not a supplement. More on forms and safety is on our magnesium evidence page.

Hair shedding: no supplement has been shown to help

A 2026 systematic review of 24 studies found hair loss reported more often with semaglutide and tirzepatide than with older GLP-1 drugs. Rapid weight loss appeared to be a contributor, and telogen effluvium, a temporary shedding that follows physical stress, was one of the main types reported.

Biotin is the supplement most often marketed for this. A systematic review of biotin for hair and nails found only 18 reported cases of benefit, and every one involved a person with an underlying deficiency or medical cause. Evidence in otherwise healthy people is limited. We found no study of biotin or collagen for hair shedding during GLP-1 treatment.

Supplements that need care with GLP-1 drugs

  • Anything that lowers blood sugar. Berberine lowers glucose in people with type 2 diabetes. The Wegovy label warns of low blood sugar when semaglutide is combined with insulin or sulfonylureas. No trial has tested berberine together with a GLP-1 drug, so tell your prescriber before combining them, especially if you take other diabetes medication. See berberine vs Ozempic.
  • Anything taken by mouth on a schedule. The label notes that semaglutide delays stomach emptying and may affect the absorption of oral medications. Whether that changes how any supplement is absorbed has not been studied.
  • Laxative herbs. Diarrhea and vomiting are already common on these drugs, and the label warns that the resulting fluid loss can injure the kidneys. Stimulant laxative herbs such as senna can add to fluid loss.

What we found no evidence for

  • Electrolyte products for people on GLP-1 drugs. We found no clinical study. Staying hydrated matters when vomiting or diarrhea occurs, but that is not evidence for a specific product.
  • "GLP-1 support" blends and supplements claimed to raise the body's own GLP-1. We found no randomized trial of any such product alongside these drugs.
  • Collagen for muscle or hair during GLP-1 treatment. Not studied.

Vitamin B12 mixed into compounded semaglutide is covered separately: see semaglutide with B12.

Common questions

Should I take creatine on semaglutide or tirzepatide?

No trial answers this directly. Creatine adds lean tissue when combined with resistance training in older adults, so it is a reasonable question to raise with your clinician if you are doing strength training. It is not a substitute for training or adequate protein.

How much of the weight lost on GLP-1 drugs is muscle?

In trials that measured it, roughly a quarter of weight lost came from lean mass, with wide variation between studies. In SURMOUNT-1 the proportion was the same for tirzepatide and placebo.

Which magnesium is used for constipation?

The trial evidence and the gastroenterology guideline are for magnesium oxide. People with kidney disease should ask a clinician first.

Does biotin stop hair loss on Ozempic or Wegovy?

There is no evidence that it does. Published benefit from biotin is limited to people with a deficiency or an underlying condition.

Can I take berberine with a GLP-1 drug?

The combination has not been tested in a trial. Both lower blood sugar, so check with your prescriber first.
