Do GLP-1 patches work? What the evidence shows

Short answer. There is no evidence that GLP-1 patches work. They contain no semaglutide, tirzepatide or any other GLP-1 drug. They are herbal stick-on patches, usually listing berberine, glutamine, cinnamon and pomegranate. We found no clinical trial of any GLP-1 patch, and no trial showing that these ingredients cause weight loss when absorbed through the skin.

A 2026 analysis in a pharmacy journal looked at 24 of these patches and concluded that none can legally be sold as a dietary supplement in the United States. A real GLP-1 drug patch does not exist yet: the drug molecule is too large to cross skin from an ordinary patch.

What a "GLP-1 patch" actually is

The patches sold online as "GLP-1 patches" or "natural GLP-1 patches" are not medicines. The 2026 analysis identified 24 patch products and one gel. On average each listed about 7 ingredients, and the four listed most often were:

  • berberine
  • glutamine or glutamate
  • cinnamon
  • pomegranate

None of these is a GLP-1 drug. The name borrows from the drug class; the contents do not.

What the 2026 analysis found

The researchers reviewed the 25 products and reported that:

  • US law defines a dietary supplement as something that is swallowed, "so all these products are illegal" as supplements;
  • no product posted a certificate of analysis, the document that shows what a batch actually contains;
  • many lacked the disclaimer the FDA requires on supplement claims;
  • many used deceptive advertising;
  • several had 1- to 2-star reviews, with customer comments suggesting they did not work or caused side effects.

Can the ingredients work through the skin?

For a patch to affect weight, its ingredients must cross the skin, reach the bloodstream in a meaningful amount, and then do something. None of those steps has been shown for these products.

  • No patch trials. We searched PubMed for trials of weight-loss or GLP-1 patches and found none.
  • Berberine through the skin is laboratory-stage. The studies we found of berberine applied to skin were laboratory and animal work on special formulations, mostly aimed at skin conditions such as psoriasis. None measured weight or blood sugar in people wearing a patch.
  • Even swallowed, the effects are modest. Taken by mouth at studied doses, berberine produced about 1 to 2 kg of weight loss in pooled trials. No patch has been shown to deliver a comparable amount into the body. See Do GLP-1 supplements work? for the ingredient-by-ingredient evidence.

Why there is no real GLP-1 drug patch

Skin is a barrier. A long-standing rule of thumb in dermatology, the "500 Dalton rule," holds that a compound must weigh under 500 Daltons to be absorbed through skin, and that "larger molecules cannot pass." Semaglutide weighs about 4,114 Daltons, roughly eight times that limit. That is why Ozempic and Wegovy are injections.

Researchers are working on microneedle patches, which use tiny dissolving needles to carry semaglutide past the skin's outer layer. The published work we found is laboratory-stage, including tests on animal skin. Nothing like it is sold as a supplement, and a stick-on herbal patch is a different thing entirely.

Risks

  • Unknown contents. With no certificate of analysis, there is no way to confirm what a patch contains or how much.
  • Side effects have been reported by buyers. The 2026 analysis noted customer comments suggesting adverse events. Any adhesive patch can also irritate the skin.
  • Delay. The larger risk is relying on a patch in place of care that works. If you have diabetes, do not use a patch in place of prescribed treatment.

Signs a product is not what it claims

  • It uses the name "GLP-1" but lists only herbs, amino acids or vitamins.
  • It promises drug-like results without a prescription.
  • It shows no certificate of analysis and no FDA disclaimer.
  • Its evidence is testimonials, not trials of the product.

Common questions

Do GLP-1 patches work for weight loss?

No trial shows that they do. We found no clinical trial of any GLP-1 patch, and their ingredients have not been shown to cause weight loss through the skin.

Do GLP-1 patches contain semaglutide or Ozempic?

No. The products analyzed in 2026 listed herbal and nutritional ingredients such as berberine, glutamine, cinnamon and pomegranate. None contained a GLP-1 drug.

Are GLP-1 patches FDA approved?

No. They are sold as dietary supplements, and a 2026 analysis concluded they do not meet the legal definition of one, because supplements must be swallowed.

Is there a real GLP-1 patch?

Not on the market that we could find. Microneedle patches that deliver semaglutide are being studied in laboratories. They are a different technology from the herbal patches sold online.

Are GLP-1 patches safe?

Their safety has not been studied. No analyzed product posted a certificate of analysis, and some buyers reported side effects.

What works instead?

Prescription GLP-1 drugs have large trials behind them and need a clinician's assessment. Among supplements taken by mouth, a few ingredients show modest effects of about 1 to 2 kg; see our review of GLP-1 supplements.
