Tirzepatide vs semaglutide: what the trials actually show

Short answer. Published head-to-head trials found greater average weight loss with tirzepatide and greater HbA1c lowering versus semaglutide 1 mg. Those results do not settle every outcome or provide a personal treatment ranking. This page is not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Which caused more weight loss in a head-to-head trial?
Tirzepatide did in SURMOUNT-5. At 72 weeks, average weight change was −20.2% with tirzepatide versus −13.7% with semaglutide in adults with obesity without type 2 diabetes. These are trial averages, not individual predictions. [PMID 40353578]

How do they compare for type 2 diabetes?
Tirzepatide lowered HbA1c more than semaglutide 1 mg in SURPASS-2. HbA1c changes were −2.01, −2.24, and −2.30 percentage points with the studied tirzepatide groups versus −1.86 with semaglutide. [PMID 34170647]

Is SURPASS-2 a comparison of the obesity doses?
No. SURPASS-2 used semaglutide 1 mg, a type 2 diabetes dose, rather than the 2.4 mg obesity dose. It does not compare the obesity dose regimens. [PMID 34170647]

Do they have the same effect on heart outcomes?
That is not established by a head-to-head heart-outcomes trial in the evidence reviewed. SELECT compared semaglutide with placebo; SURPASS-CVOT compared tirzepatide with dulaglutide. Different comparators and populations prevent a direct ranking. [PMIDs 37952131, 41406444]

What side effects were reported in the head-to-head trials?
Stomach and bowel problems were the most common adverse events in SURMOUNT-5 and were mostly mild to moderate, mainly during dose escalation. SURPASS-2 also reported nausea, diarrhea, and vomiting, primarily mild to moderate. [PMIDs 40353578, 34170647]

Are tirzepatide and semaglutide the same type of drug?
They overlap but are different. Tirzepatide activates GIP and GLP-1 receptors; semaglutide activates GLP-1 receptors only. Receptor activity alone does not establish which medicine suits an individual. [Zepbound, Mounjaro, and Wegovy labels]

How to read the evidence

  • Grade A: a published Phase 3 randomized trial, or for nutrient pages a published systematic review of randomized trials, for outcomes reported within that evidence.
  • Grade B: a Phase 2 trial, exploratory or subgroup analysis, small human trial, observational study, or labeling statement beyond a trial abstract.
  • Grade C: uncontrolled reports, animal or laboratory inference, mechanism-based predictions, and comparisons between separate trials.

What the medicines are

Tirzepatide activates GIP and GLP-1 receptors. Semaglutide activates GLP-1 receptors only. Brand indications cannot be exchanged.

Side effects within direct comparisons

Gastrointestinal events were most common and mainly mild to moderate. Both labels carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents; human relevance is unknown.

What separate studies add—and cannot settle

SURMOUNT-1 and STEP 1 each compared a drug with placebo. Those are Grade A placebo results. Using separate trials to declare a winner is Grade C because populations, duration, and methods differ.

Heart outcomes do not have a head-to-head winner here

SELECT compared semaglutide with placebo and found a hazard ratio of 0.80 for cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attack, or nonfatal stroke. SURPASS-CVOT compared tirzepatide with dulaglutide and established noninferiority, not superiority. Neither was a tirzepatide-versus-semaglutide heart-outcomes trial.

Where retatrutide fits

Retatrutide is a separate investigational medicine. Results from separate drug trials do not create a direct comparison.
